# Ipomoea purpurea A-versus-W white-locus contrast — 2026-09-25

## Why this system matters

Ipomoea purpurea contains at least two genetically distinct natural white-flower polymorphisms that produce similar achromatic floral phenotypes but differ in molecular architecture and natural frequency.

This provides a within-species contrast that is unusually informative for the floral-specific pigment decoupling hypothesis because much of the species-level ecological and phylogenetic background is shared.

## W locus — regulatory white phenotype

The W locus corresponds to the R2R3-MYB regulator IpMYB1.

Molecular evidence:
- the functional/pigmented versus nonfunctional/white alleles cosegregate with flower colour;
- white flowers show broad downregulation of core anthocyanin structural genes;
- the lesion is regulatory rather than an early structural-enzyme knockout.

Natural-frequency evidence:
- the white W-locus allele is a common polymorphism across southeastern US populations;
- literature syntheses report natural frequencies reaching approximately 0.5;
- the Del Valle et al. PAL synthesis reports I. purpurea white-petal polymorphism at 0–43%.

Pleiotropy:
- extensive work has not identified a deleterious pleiotropic fitness burden comparable to the structural A-locus white allele.

Key molecular source:
Chang et al. 2005, Genetics; IpMYB1/W-locus characterization.

## A locus — structural CHS-D white phenotype

The A locus white allele disrupts CHS-D, the chalcone synthase acting at the first committed step of the anthocyanin/flavonoid pathway.

Molecular/tissue architecture:
- coding-region insertion/loss of normal CHS-D function;
- white phenotype is associated with altered pigmentation beyond petals, including stem/vegetative tissues in the A-locus system;
- because CHS lies early in flavonoid biosynthesis, the lesion affects a broader biochemical domain than a flower-specific anthocyanin regulator.

Natural-frequency evidence:
- the A-locus white allele is exceptionally rare in natural populations;
- reviews report allele frequency never above about 0.005;
- the Del Valle et al. WAL synthesis lists the corresponding whole-plant/variegated white system as <0.005% white-petal phenotype frequency under its source-table convention.

Experimental pleiotropic cost:
- homozygous aa plants have reduced male and female fertilization success relative to AA plants at high temperature but not at low temperature;
- the experiment supports a protective role of flavonoids under heat stress.

Key source:
Coberly & Rausher 2003, Molecular Ecology, "Analysis of a chalcone synthase mutant in Ipomoea purpurea reveals a novel function for flavonoids: amelioration of heat stress."

## Same-species architecture contrast

The important comparison is not white versus pigmented flower colour. Both loci can produce white flowers.

It is:

`
regulatory floral white (W / IpMYB1)
        versus
early structural-pathway white (A / CHS-D)
`

Observed pattern:
- W-regulatory white: common polymorphism, no established comparable deleterious pleiotropic burden;
- A-structural white: extremely rare, experimentally demonstrated heat-sensitive reproductive cost.

This is consistent with the prediction that regulatory restriction of floral pigment loss can reduce pleiotropic burden relative to early systemic pathway disruption.

## Relation to Silene littorea

Silene provides a phenotype/tissue-level version of the same contrast:
- PAL: petal-specific anthocyanin loss, moderate stable local frequency;
- WAL: whole-plant anthocyanin loss, extremely low frequency.

Ipomoea adds direct locus-level molecular resolution:
- regulatory MYB white versus structural CHS white;
- common versus rare;
- weak/no demonstrated pleiotropic burden versus experimentally demonstrated heat-related reproductive cost.

The two systems therefore form complementary within-species natural experiments.

## Claim ceiling

Strongly supported:
- two white-flower loci within one species differ sharply in molecular architecture, natural frequency and known pleiotropic cost;
- the early structural CHS white allele carries experimentally demonstrated heat-related reproductive costs.

Consistent with, but not by itself proving:
- regulatory/tissue-restricted white alleles persist better because they preserve extra-floral flavonoid functions.

Do not conflate:
- allele frequency with phenotype frequency;
- the different frequency units reported in primary studies versus the Del Valle cross-species table;
- absence of detected pleiotropy at W with proof of zero pleiotropy.

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Annual-precipitation breadth and spatial organization of flower-colour variation,”** for publication as a Research Article in *Journal of Biogeography*.

Intraspecific flower-colour variation can be expressed as local coexistence of discrete variants or as geographic differentiation among populations. Although previous studies have related floral pigmentation to climatic and biotic gradients, comparative analyses have rarely treated this spatial organization of variation as a cross-species biogeographic property. Our manuscript addresses that distinction using a global, literature-derived dataset with source-level classification evidence and a reproducible, quality-filtered occurrence analysis.

The focal comparison contained 34 species from 25 families. We treated a paginated, quality-filtered GBIF sample as the main occurrence dataset and calculated species-level 95th–5th percentile breadths for eight WorldClim temperature and precipitation variables. Annual-precipitation breadth (BIO12) was the only component supported after correction across the complete eight-variable climate screen: the odds ratio for geographically structured rather than within-population variation was 0.218 (95% confidence interval = 0.080–0.593; Wald p = 0.00289; permutation p = 0.0055; Holm-adjusted permutation p = 0.0440). Every leave-one-family-out estimate remained negative. A deterministic first-page GBIF analysis retained the negative direction but did not remain supported after the same multiplicity adjustment. Open Tree and time-scaled V.PhyloMaker2 analyses also retained negative BIO12 estimates, but all phylogenetic confidence intervals included one. We therefore present the relationship as exploratory and phylogenetically unresolved rather than confirmed or causal.

We believe the manuscript is suited to *Journal of Biogeography* because its central contribution is not another general correlation between flower colour and climate. It asks why documented intraspecific variation appears as local coexistence in some species and geographic differentiation in others. The result is concentrated in the breadth of occupied annual-precipitation regimes rather than in composite moisture or temperature indices, and the manuscript explicitly distinguishes species-level realised climate occupancy from morph-specific mechanisms. The workflow, frozen classification manifest, complete eight-component screen, occurrence-sampling comparison, multiplicity correction and phylogenetic sensitivity analyses make the evidential boundaries auditable. Tables S18–S19 provide a blinded human-review template and separate rule-label key; until that review is completed, the manuscript describes the labels as source-traceable and rule-derived.

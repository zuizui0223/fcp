Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Climatic niche breadth and spatial organization of flower-colour variation,”** for publication as a Research Article in *Journal of Biogeography*.

Intraspecific phenotypic variation can be maintained as local coexistence or expressed as geographic differentiation, yet comparative studies rarely treat this spatial organization itself as a biogeographic trait. Flower-colour research has established mechanisms maintaining within-population polymorphism, geographic variation in morph frequencies, morph-specific climatic niches, and broad flower colour–climate associations. Our manuscript asks a different cross-species question: **why are colour variants documented as coexisting within populations in some species but geographically differentiated in others, and is that spatial organization associated with species-level occupied climatic niche breadth?**

We test two competing ecological expectations. A broad-gradient environmental-sorting expectation predicts that species spanning wider climatic conditions may provide greater opportunity for spatially varying selection and geographic replacement of morphs. An alternative broad-niche coexistence expectation predicts that broader ecological occupancy may accompany the local maintenance of multiple phenotypes. We evaluate these alternatives using a source-traceable frozen comparison of 34 species from 25 families (20 within-population and 14 geographically structured cases), five complementary climatic-niche metrics, and a single symmetric model specification controlling for occurrence effort.

All five primary point estimates were below one, indicating lower odds of geographically structured variation with broader sampled occupied climatic niches. Moisture breadth showed the largest observed contrast (odds ratio = 0.412, 95% confidence interval = 0.180–0.947; family-clustered Wald p = 0.0368; 9,999-permutation p = 0.0423), but support weakened after Holm adjustment (Wald p = 0.184; permutation p = 0.212). Every leave-one-family-out estimate retained the negative direction. Open Tree and time-scaled V.PhyloMaker2 analyses also retained negative point estimates for all five metrics, while all phylogenetic confidence intervals included one. CR2/Satterthwaite inference for moisture breadth gave an odds ratio of 0.407 and p = 0.063. We therefore emphasize the shared effect direction and uncertainty rather than a threshold-significant or moisture-specific mechanism.

We believe the manuscript is particularly suited to *Journal of Biogeography* because the response variable is not flower colour itself but the **spatial organization of intraspecific phenotypic diversity**. The observed direction is not straightforwardly explained by simple broad-gradient climatic sorting and instead identifies a comparative macroecological pattern that motivates future tests of fine-scale environmental variation, biotic interactions, demographic history and migration–selection balance. The paper therefore connects an established evolutionary problem — maintenance versus spatial sorting of polymorphism — with a species-level biogeographic comparison.

The evidence path and downstream analysis are explicitly auditable. The preserved literature workflow records the reduction from 1,075 retained works to 664 candidate species, a 72-species initial review queue, a 111-species resolved queue and the frozen 34-species binary comparison. The canonical downstream dataset is checksum-locked in the repository, and a single workflow reproduces the five-metric models, 9,999 permutations, leave-one-family-out analyses, collinearity diagnostics, phylogenetic sensitivities, CR2/Satterthwaite inference and finite-sample simulation. Current spatial labels remain **source-traceable and rule-derived**; the prepared blinded independent-review materials are not represented as completed human validation.

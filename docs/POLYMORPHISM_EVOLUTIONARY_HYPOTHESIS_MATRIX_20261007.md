# Flower-colour ITV, spatial differentiation and adaptation — evidence synthesis and FCP hypothesis matrix

Date: 2026-10-07 JST

## Purpose

This document reframes the FCP paper around the ecological and evolutionary meaning of intraspecific flower-colour variation (ITV), rather than around the mere recurrence of a white/nonwhite axis. It separates what prior literature establishes from what the FCP data can and cannot test.

## 1. Why flower-colour ITV matters biologically

Flower colour can affect fitness through at least two broad routes.

### A. Floral signal and mating route

Flower colour changes pollinator detection, preference, constancy and therefore pollen transfer. Major colour alleles can redirect pollinator visitation strongly, as shown by the YUP substitution in Mimulus. Flower-colour polymorphisms can also alter mating patterns and assortative pollen flow. These mechanisms can maintain within-species variation, generate population differentiation and contribute to reproductive isolation.

Key sources:
- Bradshaw HD Jr & Schemske DW. 2003. Nature 426:176–178. doi:10.1038/nature02106.
- Rodríguez-Castañeda NL & Buide ML et al. 2024. Plant Biology 26:929–943. doi:10.1111/plb.13702.
- Van der Niet T et al. 2014. pollinator-driven ecological speciation review, Annals of Botany / related special issue.
- Sapir Y et al. 2021. Frontiers in Plant Science 12:617851. doi:10.3389/fpls.2021.617851.

### B. Abiotic / pleiotropic physiological route

Anthocyanins and related flavonoids have functions outside petal signalling. Flower-colour variants can therefore covary with drought, temperature, UV/stress responses or other physiological traits, creating selection on flower colour directly or indirectly through pleiotropy.

A classic example is Linanthus parryae: a sharp flower-colour cline was not mirrored by neutral allozyme differentiation, reciprocal transplants favoured the resident morph, and environmental differences coincided with the colour boundary. Long-term work also showed temporally varying morph fitness associated with rainfall.

Key sources:
- Schemske DW & Bierzychudek P. 2007. Evolution 61:2528–2541. doi:10.1111/j.1558-5646.2007.00219.x.
- Lacey EP. 2026. American Journal of Botany 113:e70106. doi:10.1002/ajb2.70106.
- Tenhumberg B et al. 2023. Journal of Ecology. doi:10.1111/1365-2745.14057.

## 2. Why spatial structure of FCP matters

The main evolutionary distinction is not simply polymorphic versus monomorphic species. It is whether variation is maintained:

1. **within local populations** — local coexistence/balancing selection;
2. **among populations** — geographic differentiation caused by spatially varying selection, drift, restricted gene flow, colonisation history or plasticity;
3. by a mixture of both.

The Mediterranean FCP synthesis reports that polymorphic species often consist mainly of monomorphic populations plus fewer polymorphic populations, generating clinal or mosaic geographic patterns. This means species-wide colour diversity can be high even when local populations are relatively uniform.

Key source:
- Narbona E et al. 2018. Plant Biology 20(S1):8–20. doi:10.1111/plb.12575.

## 3. IBD versus IBE framework

Spatial phenotype differentiation can arise because populations are far apart (IBD-like process: restricted dispersal, drift, colonisation history), because they occupy different environments (IBE-like process: divergent selection, habitat-dependent establishment, environmentally structured gene flow), or both.

In landscape genetics, IBE is defined as genetic–environmental association that remains after geographic distance is accounted for. The same logic can be applied here only as a **phenotypic IBE-like pattern**; FCP colour distances are phenotypes, not neutral/genomic distances.

Key sources:
- Wang IJ & Bradburd GS. 2014. Molecular Ecology 23:5649–5662. doi:10.1111/mec.12938.
- Sexton JP, Hangartner SB & Hoffmann AA. 2014. Evolution 68:1–13. doi:10.1111/evo.12258.

## 4. ITV as a broader ecological property

Plant ITV can result from genetic differentiation, local adaptation, phenotypic plasticity or their combination. It can broaden niche use, alter interactions and change population/community dynamics. Therefore a species-wide trait distribution can represent a portfolio of locally expressed phenotypes rather than noise around one species mean.

Key sources:
- Bolnick DI et al. 2011. Trends in Ecology & Evolution 26:183–192. doi:10.1016/j.tree.2011.01.009.
- Westerband AC et al. 2021. Annals of Botany 127:397–410. doi:10.1093/aob/mcab011.

## 5. FCP hypothesis matrix

### H-EVO1 — distributed-polymorphism hypothesis

**Prediction:** species-wide flower-colour diversity is geographically partitioned: nearby observations are more homogeneous than expected from the species' overall colour composition.

**Test:** for each species, compare observed local-pair colour diversity with a vertex-permutation null preserving coordinates and the exact colour-state multiset. Repeat over multiple geographic radii.

**Evolutionary meaning if supported:** range-wide ITV often resides among localities rather than as ubiquitous local coexistence, consistent with clinal/mosaic polymorphism maintenance.

**Nonclaim:** does not distinguish selection from drift/gene flow/history.

### H-EVO2 — IBE-like environmental sorting

**Prediction:** environmental difference predicts flower-colour difference after geographic distance is controlled.

**Primary environmental candidate after the 500+500 screen:** BIO5.

**Test:** partial rank / matched vertex permutation; quantify both environment|geography and geography|environment to compare IBE-like and IBD-like components.

**Evolutionary meaning if supported:** spatial flower-colour differentiation is not reducible to distance alone and is consistent with environmental sorting.

**Nonclaim:** phenotype-based IBE-like pattern is not genetic IBE and does not prove local adaptation.

### H-EVO3 — directionally heterogeneous environmental response

**Prediction:** absolute BIO5 difference predicts colour turnover across species, while one common signed rule such as warmer = white fails to transport consistently.

**Current evidence before the new decomposition:** BIO5 colour-distance sorting is positive in discovery, validation and the third cohort; the earlier signed white-minus-nonwhite BIO5 rule failed its frozen cross-cohort transport criterion.

**Evolutionary meaning if supported:** environmental response is repeatable in magnitude/turnover but heterogeneous in direction across species.

### H-EVO4 — environmental heterogeneity generates greater total ITV

**Prediction:** species spanning more heterogeneous climates have greater species-wide D.

**Current evidence:** the broad multivariate prediction failed joint 500+500 support; solar heterogeneity was selected in the 500+500 screen but failed the fixed third-cohort transport (partial rho 0.0171, p=0.382).

**Current status:** not supported as a general explanation of species-wide D.

### H-EVO5 — local differentiation can be a precursor to reproductive isolation

**Prediction from prior theory:** if geographically differentiated flower colours also alter pollinator constancy/mating, spatial FCP can contribute to reproductive isolation and potentially speciation.

**FCP testability:** not directly testable with current photo data. The present dataset can identify the geographic phenotype structure that would make this process possible but cannot measure assortative mating or gene flow.

### H-EVO6 — adaptation versus plasticity

**Prediction:** environment-associated phenotype sorting can arise from genetic local adaptation or repeated plastic response.

**FCP testability:** unresolved. Common-garden, reciprocal-transplant or genomic data are required to distinguish them.

## 6. Claim ladder

The FCP data can potentially support, in increasing order:

1. **spatial structure** — nearby flowers are more similar;
2. **distributed polymorphism** — local colour diversity is depleted relative to species-wide composition;
3. **phenotypic IBE-like sorting** — environmental distance predicts colour distance beyond geography;
4. **environment-specific local adaptation** — requires fitness/genetic evidence, not available here;
5. **reproductive isolation/speciation** — requires mating/gene-flow evidence, not available here.

The manuscript should stop at the highest rung actually supported by replicated FCP analyses.

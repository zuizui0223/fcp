# Temperature × pigment-network bidirectional coupling — FCP mechanism synthesis

## Key update

The mechanistic model should not be reduced to:

`high temperature -> anthocyanin suppression -> white`.

Published factorial genetics shows the reverse causal arrow also exists:

`pigment-pathway state -> heat response / fitness`.

### Ipomoea purpurea: CHS genotype × temperature

Coberly & Rausher (2003) experimentally compared pigmented `AA` and white `aa` plants at the A locus. The white allele contains an Ac/Ds insertion in `CHS-D`, preventing a functional transcript. Experimental genetic backgrounds were randomized using two crossing/inbreeding lines.

The experiment crossed temperature, light, maternal genotype, paternal genotype and line.

Temperature treatments:
- low: 27/18 C day/night;
- high: 36/24 C initially, then 32/20 C.

Drought confounding was deliberately minimized by humidity control and twice-daily saturation watering.

For fertilization success:
- maternal genotype × maternal temperature: F=7.32, p=0.0069;
- paternal genotype × maternal temperature: F=4.85, p=0.0278;
- at high temperature, white `aa` recipients had ~26% lower fertilization success than pigmented `AA`;
- white pollen had ~24% lower success than pigmented pollen on high-temperature recipients;
- at low temperature, these genotype differences were absent.

Thus an early flavonoid-pathway knockout makes reproductive function more vulnerable to heat.

## Complement with Moricandia

The public Moricandia experiment establishes the complementary arrow:

- same 15 individuals, mild summer 23.75 C -> hot summer 28.75 C at the same summer photoperiod;
- mean floral cyanidin falls 1.893 -> 0.531 (~71.9% reduction);
- 13/15 individuals decrease; paired p=0.000854;
- anthocyanin rebounds when plants are returned toward spring conditions;
- the spring -> mild-summer white transition is accompanied by lower MYB90, CHS, U75C1/U78D2 and TTG1.

So there are at least two experimentally supported causal directions:

`
temperature -> pigment-network output
pigment-network genotype -> thermal reproductive performance
`

## Revised FCP mechanism

A better model is a coupled reaction norm:

`
                     temperature
                    /           \
                   v             v
pigment genotype/regulation -> pathway flux -> flower colour
                   \             |
                    \------------> thermal performance / fitness
`

The resulting flower-colour phenotype can therefore reflect both:
1. environmental plasticity in pathway flux;
2. inherited pathway state;
3. selection on the pleiotropic thermal consequences of that pathway state.

This immediately explains why no universal BIO5 coefficient is expected. High temperature can reduce pigmentation plastically in one system while simultaneously selecting against a pigment-deficient genotype in another.

## FCP implication

The recurrent white/nonwhite axis may be a recurrent **low-flux state of a conserved pigment network**, but the ecological sign of temperature is contingent on where the network is perturbed and what physiological functions are coupled to the affected metabolites.

This shifts the general hypothesis from:

> heat causes repeated white flowers

to:

> a conserved pigment network creates a recurrent achromatic endpoint, and temperature interacts with that network in genotype- and system-dependent ways.

That hypothesis is compatible with:
- the strong global white/nonwhite phenotype geometry;
- repeated molecular convergence on the pigment network;
- strong Moricandia heat-induced depigmentation;
- the Ipomoea CHS-null × heat fitness interaction;
- failure of BIO5 to replicate as a universal cross-species effect.

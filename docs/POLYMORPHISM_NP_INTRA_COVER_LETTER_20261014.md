# New Phytologist — one within-species flower-colour variation Full Paper cover letter

**Status:** Working draft. Corresponding author, all authors and coauthor approval remain required. The existing high-depth analysis and frozen scientific decisions are unchanged.

**To the Editors of *New Phytologist*,**

Please consider our Full Paper, **“Recurrent achromatic–chromatic variation and geographic allocation of flower colours within species”**, for publication.

The study addresses a question that is distinct from mapping average flower colour across different species: **does flower-colour variation *within* a species occupy recurrent directions in phenotype space, and how are its observed states allocated geographically?** We assembled high-depth, repeatedly photographed source distributions for 1,499 species (149,900 photographs) and separated measurement reliability, colour-axis discovery and prospective confirmation before asking the ecological spatial questions.

Three linked contributions underpin the paper. First, measured species-wide colour diversity can be reproduced across observer-disjoint partitions and fresh photographic IDs, allowing a visible phenotype *distribution* rather than a single modal category to be compared. Second, continuous within-species colour variation repeatedly concentrates on an achromatic–chromatic direction: the originally identified axis was frozen and tested in a newly sampled 158-species cohort, yielding W = **0.517** versus a construction-preserving null median **0.457** (P = **0.001**). Third, local conspecific photographic samples are more colour-homogeneous than exact species-range composition nulls across three species-disjoint source cohorts (50-km mean depletion **0.02053/0.01867/0.01468**, each P = **0.005**).

The biological implications are informative but bounded. A species-wide photo-colour distribution can reflect local mixing and among-locality differentiation. Recurrent white–coloured geometry does not require one conserved pigment mutation, and neither geographic structuring nor nominal temperature–photo-colour association proves climate selection, genetic polymorphism maintenance or relative flower-colour fitness. The white photographic category is coupled to image exposure; the manuscript explicitly reports this technical sensitivity and the frozen INDETERMINATE validity decision, while documenting that the structured-null result persists after high-clip exclusion.

For the journal's editorial assessment:

**1. What hypotheses or questions does the work address?**  
Can high-depth photographic measurements recover reproducible flower-colour distributions within species, do those distributions show a shared achromatic–chromatic direction, and how does their sampled geographical allocation differ from unrestricted local mixing?

**2. How does the work advance plant science?**  
Across 1,499 sampled plant species, we combine observer-disjoint repeatability and a species/photo-disjoint prospective colour-space test with fixed-composition geographical comparisons. The confirmed axis and replicated spatial allocation are separate, biologically informative properties of within-species floral variation.

**3. Why is the work important and timely?**  
Photographic biodiversity archives increasingly support global trait research. A single species-level colour label misses both within-species phenotype geometry and spatial arrangement. This study supplies a reproducible comparative approach and clear evidence thresholds for future genomic, pigmentation and fitness investigations.

The work is one **within-species** New Phytologist paper; a separate, entirely interspecific global flower-colour × moisture source maintained at `zuizui0223/mois` is not presented as a within-species biological replication. The 42,111-species metadata frame is only a photographic opportunity sample.

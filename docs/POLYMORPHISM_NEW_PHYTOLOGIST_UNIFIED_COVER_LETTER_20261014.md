# Cover letter — single unified New Phytologist Full Paper (working draft, 14 October 2026)

**Submission status:** draft only; author metadata, submission archive and final Figure 6 artwork remain incomplete.

**To the Editors of New Phytologist,**

Please consider our manuscript, **“Geographic partitioning of flower-colour variation is widespread, while climatic associations are scale dependent,”** as a single Full Paper.

The study combines two complementary scales of plant floral variation: a validated high-depth, species-disjoint study of the spatial distribution and phenotype-space direction of intraspecific floral colour, and an observational worldwide environmental extension that tests whether broad climatic colour correlations persist under stricter spatial, taxonomic and independent colour-measurement requirements. Together these layers make a unified ecological claim while explicitly distinguishing geographic pattern from its evolutionary causes.

Our central result is positive and replicated: nearby conspecific flower-colour observations are less mixed than each species' exact range-wide colour composition predicts across three independent species cohorts within one photographic source system. A prospectively frozen achromatic–chromatic colour-space axis also survives a construction-preserving null. The macroecological analysis finds broad climatic colour correspondence but not a robust worldwide directional rule once key observation and spatial sensitivities are considered. We do not interpret environmental correlation as climate-induced natural selection or evidence of balancing selection.

As required for editorial assessment, we answer the journal's three questions below. These responses concern **one manuscript**, not companion submissions.

**1. What hypotheses or questions does this work address?**  
Does species-wide flower-colour variation coexist locally or partition geographically, and does it occupy recurrent colour-space directions? We then ask whether global photographic colour–climate relationships remain consistent when plant taxa, spatial scale, image quality and independently described botanical colour are taken into account.

**2. How does this work advance our current understanding of plant science?**  
We show that geographic partitioning of floral variation recurs across three species-disjoint samples under each species' exact colour-composition null, while a prospectively frozen colour-space axis also recurs. Worldwide climatic associations are demonstrably conditional, separating comparative floral pattern from unsupported universal evolutionary mechanisms.

**3. Why is this work important and timely?**  
Large photographic biodiversity resources increasingly support global trait maps, but sampling, spatial history and environmental covariation can masquerade as shared adaptive rules. This study establishes a repeatable spatial phenotype pattern and quantifies where broader climatic explanations cease to transfer, improving inference from macroecological plant-colour data.

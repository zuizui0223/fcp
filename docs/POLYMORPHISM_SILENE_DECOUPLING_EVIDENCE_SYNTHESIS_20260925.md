### Published main-text result
The phenotype-specific tissue interpretation therefore remains source-derived from the peer-reviewed article rather than newly recalculated from Table S3.

The published study reports:
- PAL plants retain anthocyanins in photosynthetic tissues despite white petals;
- WAL plants lack anthocyanins broadly across petals and photosynthetic tissues;
- PAL and WAL plants retain similar flavone content to fully pigmented plants;
- PAL petal anthocyanin loss is consistent with petal-specific downregulation near F3H / SlMyb1a.

This source-level statement is not relabelled as an independent public-data reanalysis.

## Mechanistic interpretation

The combined evidence distinguishes two stages.

### Origin
Both petal-restricted and whole-plant anthocyanin-loss variants can arise.

WAL is detected in more S. littorea populations than PAL, so the evidence does not support a claim that petal-specific variants necessarily originate more often.

### Persistence
Once present, PAL can persist locally at moderate frequency whereas WAL remains extremely rare.

The same qualitative frequency separation appears across the 13 PAL versus 13 WAL systems assembled in the source paper.

This supports a **maintenance-filter** version of floral-specific pigment decoupling:

> restricting anthocyanin loss to petals can permit a depigmented floral phenotype while retaining extra-floral pigment functions, increasing the conditions under which white flowers can persist at appreciable frequency.

## Claim ceiling

Strongly supported as a descriptive natural pattern:
- petal-restricted anthocyanin-loss phenotypes are reported at much higher natural frequencies than whole-plant anthocyanin-loss mutants.

Supported as a mechanistic interpretation:
- preserving extra-floral anthocyanin function is a plausible reason tissue-restricted white phenotypes can persist.

Not demonstrated:
- tissue restriction is the sole causal driver of frequency;
- mutation rates are equal;
- source studies form an unbiased meta-analytic sample;
- all white-flower systems follow this architecture.

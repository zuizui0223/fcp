# BIO5 × pigment regulation: one bounded explanation for shared FCP differentiation

**18 October 2026 — source-linked mechanistic interpretation for the active New Phytologist FCP manuscript.** This is an evidence synthesis, **not a new fitted model** and **not an established cross-species selection mechanism**. Its purpose is to connect the existing signed BIO5 result and anthocyanin-pathway results to the photographic FCP composition/geography question without multiplying hypotheses.

## The single testable mechanistic proposition

**The shared feature may be a two-step constraint, not a common optimal flower hue:** a conserved *pigment-producing network* permits multiple floral phenotypes with different levels of pleiotropy; local environmental and demographic contexts influence the spatial frequencies of those phenotypes. The molecular *step and colour sign* can vary among species. Accordingly, a universal 'warm BIO5 produces white' or 'all white flowers share F3H loss' prediction is **not** implied.

FCP data currently identify photographed state unevenness (3,297 classifiable taxa) and species-conditional geographic allocation (1,761 local-pair eligible taxa), **not** inherited colour-morph frequencies or their selective equilibrium. The original chronologically frozen W confirmation is an achromatic–chromatic *image-space alignment* finding, **not proof of anthocyanin chemistry, pathway loss/reacquisition or genetic maintenance**.

## Existing BIO5 evidence: real positives, real falsification

BIO5 is **maximum temperature of the warmest month** (WorldClim 2.1), not an observed plant-stress experiment or vegetation-tissue temperature.

## Molecular-route exploration: pathway-level recurrence, not a universal gene

The **anthocyanin/flavonoid biosynthetic network** can be changed at structural enzymes (such as CHS, F3H, F3′H, DFR, ANS) or through regulatory factors (notably floral R2R3-MYB and other MYB–bHLH–WD40 components). Tissue-restricted changes may preserve extra-floral pigment functions, and different mutations can alter white/pigmented morph frequencies in different ways. Importantly, *white/yellow/blue/red colour-state labels from photographs do not chemically identify an anthocyanin pathway state*; carotenoids, tissue pH, photonic structure, flower ageing and image conditions also matter.

**PAL here means *petal anthocyanin loss*, not the phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) enzyme.** These must never be confused.

**Critical missing direct connection:** the taxonomically normalised PAL/WAL literature intersects the originally analysed FCP image cohorts with only **two petal-loss and two whole-plant-loss natural examples**, all in the original validation/reserve cohort. No valid inferential PAL-vs-WAL × D or local-50-km comparison is possible, and no molecular-pathway genotype has been assigned to the 4,475 photo-classified taxa. ## One falsifiable research implication; no expansion of the active paper's tested claims

The *conserved* element is potentially the **ability of the flavonoid network to be modified while sparing essential pigment functions outside petals**, not one universally selected gene or a common temperature direction. Environmental heterogeneity is a potential **context for spatial morph sorting**; regulatory tissue specificity is a potential **constraint on which variants persist**. Their interaction has **not** been measured directly in the current FCP image dataset.

A future biologically validated FCP comparison would (a) score inherited, same-organ colour morphs in independent plants/populations; (b) identify genetic/pigment mechanisms and whether anthocyanin changes are petal-restricted versus systemic; (c) estimate replicated local morph frequencies, abiotic conditions, gene flow and morph-specific fitness; then (d) test whether tissue restriction predicts *persistence* after accounting for ecology, demographic history and observer/phylogeny. A putative climate effect would require the **same-site-season and independent-photo-provider** controls that the current BIO5 result lacks.

**Publication ceiling:** main New Phytologist evidence = measured within-species photo-colour composition + source-conditional geographic allocation + original frozen excess achromatic–chromatic W. BIO5 = supported initial environmental association with failed signed transport and weak technical controls. Molecular = published single-species and selected-literature comparative **plausibility**, not 4,475-taxon functional-genetic evidence. Do not call any of these a proved universal ecological/genetic FCP diversification principle.

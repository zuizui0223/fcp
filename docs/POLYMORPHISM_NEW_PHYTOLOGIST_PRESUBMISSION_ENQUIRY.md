# New Phytologist presubmission enquiry draft — flower-colour polymorphism

**Working manuscript title**

A recurrent achromatic–chromatic axis structures within-species flower-colour polymorphism across plant species

## What is the question addressed?

Can within-species flower-colour polymorphism be measured reproducibly as a species-level phenotype across many plant species, and—once measurement validity is separated from observer effects—does the geometry of that variation repeatedly align with a common direction in colour space?

## What is the principal advance?

We separate measurement validation, target discovery and prospective confirmation.

First, a continuous four-state polymorphism score was reproducible across 200 observer-disjoint reserve partitions (median Spearman rho = 0.789; fifth percentile = 0.765). Second, the original discovery and reserve cohorts localized construction-controlled recurrent colour geometry to a fixed white-versus-nonwhite axis rather than a general shared hue axis. Because that named axis was identified after the original geometry had been opened, we froze it as a target and tested it prospectively in a new species-disjoint cohort.

The prospective cohort completed 49,900 terminal measurements from 499 species, with 377 species passing the predeclared measurement-support gate. The unchanged white-axis statistic was supported at both the primary tier (158 species, W = 0.51725, structured-null p = 0.001) and the stricter prespecified tier (86 species, W = 0.53293, p = 0.001). No species were replaced and the axis, thresholds, statistic and structured null were not refitted after biological opening.

## Why is the work important and timely?

Large community-science image collections increasingly make it possible to study trait distributions rather than only species means, but scale alone does not establish that image-derived intraspecific variation is a reproducible biological phenotype. Our study provides a general workflow for separating observer-level measurement validity, target discovery and prospective species-disjoint confirmation, and identifies a recurrent achromatic–chromatic geometry of flower-colour polymorphism across hundreds of plant species.

The result is deliberately bounded. The prospective test is species-disjoint but comes from the same iNaturalist source/opportunity universe and uses the same measurement system, so we do not describe it as an independent-source replication. We also do not infer pigment chemistry, evolutionary direction, pollinator selection or climatic adaptation from the colour-space axis.

## Editorial fit

The manuscript is plant-wide, question-driven and centred on intraspecific phenotypic variation and recurrent colour geometry rather than on a single taxon, location or environmental association. Its strongest contribution is a prospectively confirmed plant phenotypic regularity together with a validation architecture that makes the evidence status of that regularity explicit.

# Spatiotemporal turnover prior-art boundary — v0.1

## Purpose

This audit defines what the generalized programme may and may not claim before any generalized turnover outcome is opened.

## Established components

Several ingredients are already established and therefore are not priority claims:

- geographic distance-decay of ecological similarity and beta diversity;
- global comparisons of taxonomic and functional spatial turnover;
- phylogenetic niche conservatism and trait retention across evolutionary history;
- phylogenetic signal in ecological-network architecture;
- stronger phylogenetic signal in some antagonistic than mutualistic network components;
- interaction beta diversity and the decomposition of species turnover versus rewiring;
- environment-driven rewiring of ecological networks.

Key references include Morlon et al. 2008 (Ecology Letters, DOI 10.1111/j.1461-0248.2008.01202.x), Crisp & Cook 2012 (New Phytologist, DOI 10.1111/j.1469-8137.2012.04298.x), Rohr & Bascompte 2014 (American Naturalist, DOI 10.1086/678234), and Ward et al. 2026 (Nature Reviews Biodiversity, DOI 10.1038/s44358-026-00159-9).

## Working gap

The programme is not built around a new distance-decay statistic.

The working gap is the combination of:

1. the same null-centered turnover effect convention for evolutionary and geographic separation;
2. the same finite biological predictor family across both axes;
3. explicit application to both organismal traits and interaction identity;
4. mandatory source independence between predictor and response;
5. interaction-space decomposition so species replacement cannot masquerade as rewiring;
6. prospective qualification and abstention before biological outcomes are opened.

The strongest defensible novelty target is therefore:

> A common response and predictor architecture for asking why biological information is retained or lost across evolutionary time and geographic space, tested for both traits and biotic interactions.

This is a working novelty hypothesis, not a priority claim.

## 2026 rewiring review boundary

Ward et al. 2026 explicitly synthesizes ecological-network rewiring in variable environments and argues for integrating traits with dynamic network thinking.

Accordingly, this programme must not claim that linking traits, environmental variation and rewiring is itself new.

The narrower test here is whether the same predeclared system properties explain turnover on evolutionary and geographic axes under one matched effect convention.

## PNC boundary

Crisp & Cook emphasize that phylogenetic niche conservatism is a pattern rather than a single process and varies among traits and lineages.

Accordingly, temporal-memory effects are treated as responses to explain, not as evidence for one universal conservative mechanism.

## Network phylogeny boundary

Rohr & Bascompte already show phylogenetic signal in network structure and differences among interaction types.

Accordingly, interaction type is a predeclared moderator, and phylogenetic organization of interactions is not claimed as a new phenomenon.

## Spatial turnover boundary

Distance-decay and spatial beta diversity are mature literatures. The programme therefore treats geographic turnover as one matched axis, not as a newly discovered pattern.

## Hard nonclaims

- not the first study of distance-decay;
- not the first study of phylogenetic trait conservatism;
- not the first study of phylogenetic structure in ecological networks;
- not the first study of network rewiring;
- not the first decomposition of interaction turnover;
- not evidence that one mechanism controls time and space unless the common-driver tests pass;
- not evidence for a universal turnover law.

## Current status

No generalized trait or interaction turnover outcome has been opened for this programme.

# FCP: regional generality of geographically allocated flower-colour ITV
## Post-outcome independent ecological extension — 2026-10-08

### The main comparative question

The **strongest confirmed FCP ecological pattern** is spatial allocation of flower-colour states inside a species' sampled geographic range, relative to that species' own fixed visible-colour composition. The established 50-km mean local discordance depletion is 0.020529 / 0.018673 / 0.014685 for discovery / validation / third (P=0.005 each). A source-frozen extension (PR #129, successful Actions 37736541255) preserved month-specific photographed colour counts, and mean residual 50-km depletion remained +0.017455 / +0.015287 / +0.013324 (P=0.005 each, 3 species-disjoint cohorts).

By contrast, a separate source-frozen latitude/elevation atlas (PR #127, successful Actions 37739944082) found **no latitude or elevation band with a statistically transported signed white-fraction or diversity deviation in all three species cohorts**. Some 0–15° and 60–90° bin comparisons were too sparsely replicated within the same species to estimate a trend.

These results are not contradictory. **The next ecological question is where the *spatial structure itself* recurs geographically, rather than whether a specific flower colour is universally more frequent at high altitude or latitude.**

### Latitude regions and exact meaning

For each species, use ONLY its photographs inside a given absolute-latitude zone; compute local versus species-within-that-zone colour variation. Thus region-specific effects do not inherit distant species-wide diversity differences or local photo pairs crossing two climate bands. The same species can be examined independently in multiple regions when its photo sample supports it; region comparisons are not presumed independent.

Primary, fixed geographic bands:
- low latitude 0–30° (includes tropics AND subtropics, NOT equivalent to all tropical climatic conditions);
- middle latitude 30–60°;
- high latitude 60–90°.

Boundary-based sensitivity, not a substitution after viewing outcomes:
- astronomical tropics 0–23.5°;
- 23.5–60°;
- 60–90°.

No data-derived bin-width optimization. Use both hemispheres via absolute latitude, and disclose northern/southern sampling imbalance.

### Coverage and generality contract

For EACH regional effect:
- require >=30 geographically evaluable species and >=25 conditionally identifiable species;
- both unrestricted and different-observer local-pair modes must be positive, with 999-bootstrap lower species CI >0 and 199-permutation P significant after **Holm adjustment over the 3 regional bands × 2 observer policies** within cohort;
- to claim transport within a region, ALL three disjoint species cohorts must satisfy the same conditions. The 0–23.5° astronomical tropical sensitivity has the same diagnostic boundaries, but does not replace the primary 0–30° low-latitude region;
- high-latitude coverage shortage must produce HOLD, not a claimed zero effect or a stronger universal law from absence of observed evidence.

Report for every cohort-region:
- source species+photos, dated subset and northern/southern photographic split;
- species with any local regional photo data vs >=20 dates vs >=30 near and distant pairs;
- species/photograph/independent-genera counts for the actual estimator;
- conditionally identifiable count, measured local depletion, conditional null mean, their residual, uncertainty and both observer modes.

Do NOT test a difference between two regions from the separate unpaired mean estimates, as their species pools differ. A within-the-same-species matched regional comparison would be a separate estimand requiring taxa with adequate photo opportunity in BOTH regions; its feasibility can be reported later.

### Interpretation boundaries

- These are **photographically sampled conspecific neighbourhoods** within latitudinal regions, not genetically verified mating populations or a field-selected sample of all tropical or boreal plants.
- The three species groups are taxonomically disjoint but share the same photo source and colour classifier, so the result generalizes *within that source's sampled opportunity frame* only.
- Visible white flower labels correlate with image highlight/exposure and may not equal chemically pigmentless petals; four-state labels are colour categories, not anthocyanin loci.
- Species and plant-observation frequencies differ across geographic regions; neither region-based raw species count nor photo frequency estimates the natural prevalence of FCP in all world flora.
- Calendar month can be confounded with phenology, observer, daylight, and different seasonal timing in northern versus southern latitudes.
- Regional geographic colour sorting is not evidence of pollinator-mediated fitness, local adaptation, genetic population structure or a shared environmental mechanism.
- Existing frozen main paper H1/H2/50km results and PR #127/#129 results are never rewritten to fit this new post hoc regional hypothesis.

The central ecological value of this project is the **scale and scope of replicated spatial allocation**. If low and middle latitudes replicate but high latitudes are unidentifiable, the correct conclusion is a supported tested domain plus a high-latitude opportunity gap—not a universal theorem for flowering plants.

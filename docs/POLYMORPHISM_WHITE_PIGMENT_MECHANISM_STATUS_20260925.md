## Manual adjudication of the three A/B candidates

### Aquilegia vulgaris — mechanistically relevant, not strict natural-FCP evidence

A purple-flowered and a white-flowered plant were genome-sequenced. Structural variation in an anthocyanidin synthase (ANS) gene was identified as a plausible explanation for the white phenotype.

The study describes A. vulgaris as an ornamental plant and does not establish in its abstract that the compared individuals represent a naturally segregating wild polymorphism.

Decision:
- anthocyanin-pathway mechanism: **supported as a species mechanism candidate**
- strict natural-FCP Tier A/B: **not verified**

### Cyclamen graecum — strongest proximal mechanism candidate, natural provenance unresolved

The white-flowered variant had lower anthocyanin amounts and higher flavonols than the pink-purple wild type. DFR2 expression was lower in the white variant, whereas other key anthocyanin-pathway genes changed little. The authors infer a defect in CgraDFR2 expression as the likely white-flower mechanism.

The accessible abstract establishes a white-flowered variant versus a wild-type plant but does not by itself establish that the white variant is a naturally segregating wild-population morph.

Decision:
- anthocyanin reduction: **directly supported**
- regulatory/expression mechanism at DFR2: **directly supported**
- strict natural-FCP Tier A/B: **provenance unresolved**

### Orychophragmus violaceus — direct ANS mechanism, artificial mapping system

The study crossed a purebred white mutant line and a purple line, built F2/BC1 mapping populations and identified the white locus. OvANS expression and promoter variation/inactivation were implicated.

Decision:
- anthocyanin/ANS white mechanism: **directly supported**
- strict natural-FCP Tier A/B: **excluded from natural evidence because the causal inference uses mutant/breeding populations**

## Synthesis

All three manually inspected high-priority species point to the **anthocyanin biosynthetic pathway**, specifically ANS or DFR-level loss/reduction/regulation.

That convergence is biologically informative, but the third-cohort panel has very sparse direct natural molecular evidence. None of the three can currently be promoted to a strict, verified natural-population Tier A result from the accessible source evidence alone.

This is a **coverage limitation, not evidence against anthocyanin convergence**. The 281-species third cohort is a random high-depth photographic validation panel and does not contain several classic natural flower-colour genetic systems such as Ipomoea purpurea, Raphanus sativus or Linanthus parryae.

## Next move

The proximal-mechanism audit should therefore expand from the photographic third cohort to the project's literature-derived natural-FCP species universe. That panel is specifically enriched for biologically documented polymorphic systems and is the appropriate place to ask whether natural white/pigmented differences repeatedly map onto anthocyanin regulatory or structural changes.

The New Phytologist manuscript remains unchanged.

# Floral-specific pigment decoupling — evidence synthesis after Silene PAL/WAL reanalysis

Date: 2026-09-25

## Current evidence hierarchy

### Level 1 — recurrent phenotype-space geometry
The FCP programme shows recurrent white/nonwhite achromatic–chromatic alignment across species, but not a common global geographic boundary and not a confirmed nonwhite->white evolutionary transition direction.

### Level 2 — pathway-level molecular convergence
Natural white/pigmented systems repeatedly implicate reduced or rerouted floral pigment output through different nodes of the anthocyanin/flavonoid network, including MYB, CHS, F3H, ANS/UFGT and MBW-related regulation.

### Level 3 — environmental perturbation can hit the same network
Moricandia arvensis provides a direct within-species temperature-dose bridge:
- hotter summer under the same stated summer photoperiod reduces floral cyanidin by ~72%;
- returning toward spring conditions restores cyanidin;
- summer-white flowers downregulate MYB90, CHS, U75C1/U78D2 and TTG1;
- kaempferol-equivalent flavonol is broadly maintained.

This demonstrates a system-specific thermal route through the same pigment network, without establishing a universal BIO5 effect.

### Level 4 — within-species natural decoupling and persistence
Silene littorea distinguishes:
- PAL: petal anthocyanin loss with anthocyanins retained in photosynthetic tissues;
- WAL: whole-plant anthocyanin loss.

Mechanical reanalysis of Supplementary Table S2 across 21 natural populations gives:
- PAL: 2 populations, 6 positive population-years, positive frequencies 8–21%, median 15.5%; both populations positive across three repeated survey years.
- WAL: 9 populations, 17 positive population-years, positive frequencies 0.05–0.86%, median 0.21%.

Thus PAL is geographically restricted but can persist at moderate local frequency, whereas WAL appears in more populations yet remains extremely rare.

The positive-frequency median contrast is 73.8-fold. More importantly, the smallest positive PAL frequency (8%) exceeds the largest positive WAL frequency (0.86%).

### Level 5 — cross-species PAL/WAL literature frequency scale
Mechanical reanalysis of Supplementary Table S1 gives 13 PAL and 13 WAL species.

PAL:
- all 13 have numeric frequency information;
- documented maximum white frequency: minimum 10%, median 43%, maximum 100%.

WAL:
- 9 numeric systems and 4 reported qualitatively as rare/extremely rare;
- numeric upper bounds: 0.001–1.4%, median upper bound 0.1%.

The conservative frequency scales do not overlap:
- minimum PAL documented maximum = 10%;
- maximum numeric WAL upper bound = 1.4%;
- conservative separation factor = 7.14.

No p-value is used because source studies differ in survey design and reporting format.

## Strongest mechanistic interpretation

The most coherent model is not simply that white flowers are mutationally easy.

It is:

> white floral phenotypes are more likely to persist when pigment loss is developmentally decoupled from extra-floral flavonoid functions.

A flower- or petal-restricted regulatory change can lower floral anthocyanin while preserving anthocyanin/flavonoid functions in leaves, stems, calyces or other tissues. By contrast, whole-plant pigment-loss variants may arise repeatedly but remain at very low frequency because of pleiotropic costs, linked physiological effects, or other correlated disadvantages.

The Silene evidence is especially informative because PAL and WAL occur within the same species, reducing many cross-species confounders.

## Important nuance: origin versus persistence

The data do not show that PAL mutations arise more often than WAL mutations.

Indeed, in S. littorea WAL is detected in more populations than PAL.

The stronger inference is about persistence/frequency after appearance:

`
origin of depigmented variant
        ->
architecture of pigment loss
        ->
pleiotropic burden / ecological performance
        ->
frequency and persistence
`

Thus the hypothesis is fundamentally a **maintenance filter**, not necessarily a mutation-rate hypothesis.

## Relation to the failed universal temperature effect

This model explains why cross-species BIO5 need not replicate.

Temperature can:
- suppress floral anthocyanin and generate white plasticity when the pigment response is flower-restricted or branch-specific;
- impose a cost on systemic flavonoid-loss genotypes under stress;
- have different phenotypic signs depending on pathway position, genotype and tissue specificity.

Therefore a common molecular network can coexist with heterogeneous ecological coefficients.

## What is not yet established

The stricter molecular pleiotropic-cost comparison remains underpowered.

The frozen molecular-architecture preflight currently has:
- DEC: 3 strict mechanism instances across 3 families;
- SYS: 3 strict mechanism instances across 3 families.

The prespecified gate requires >=5 mechanism instances and >=3 families in each class. Therefore a formal mechanism-class versus pleiotropic-cost comparative test is not authorized and the threshold is not lowered.

The broad PAL/WAL frequency synthesis supports the hypothesis at the phenotype/tissue-scope level; it does not replace the missing causal-molecular comparative panel.

## Current claim ceiling

Supported:
> Across natural systems assembled in the published literature, petal-restricted anthocyanin loss is reported at much higher frequencies than whole-plant anthocyanin-loss variants; within S. littorea the same contrast is reproduced across repeated population surveys. Together with molecular and thermal pathway evidence, this is consistent with floral-specific pigment decoupling facilitating persistence of achromatic floral phenotypes.

Not supported:
- tissue restriction is the sole causal determinant of frequency;
- all white flowers arise by this mechanism;
- systemic loss always lowers fitness;
- temperature is the universal selective driver;
- nonwhite->white transitions are evolutionarily predominant.

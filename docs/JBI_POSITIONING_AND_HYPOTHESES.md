# JBI positioning: theory, novelty, hypotheses and ecological interpretation

## Decision

**Primary target: Journal of Biogeography — Research Article.**

The paper should be positioned as a comparative biogeographic study of **the spatial organization of intraspecific phenotypic variation**, using flower colour as a tractable discrete trait. It should not be positioned primarily as another flower-colour–climate paper or as a conventional meta-analysis.

## 1. What is already established

### 1.1 Within-population polymorphism and among-population differentiation are biologically distinct spatial states

Flower-colour polymorphism is conventionally defined as the coexistence of at least two discrete flower-colour variants within the same population (Narbona et al. 2018). Reviews of plant balancing selection distinguish mechanisms that maintain variation within populations from mechanisms that maintain differentiation among populations. In particular, negative frequency-dependent selection can maintain within-population polymorphism, whereas spatially varying selection can maintain adaptive differentiation among populations; migration can oppose or redistribute that differentiation (Delph & Kelly 2014).

This provides the theoretical basis for treating `within_population` and `among_population` as more than descriptive labels. They are alternative **spatial organizations of intraspecific variation** that can arise under different combinations of balancing selection, spatially varying selection, migration, drift, demographic history and biotic interactions.

### 1.2 Flower-colour variation has multiple selective agents

Flower-colour evolution cannot be reduced to pollinator preference. Narbona et al. (2018) reviewed pollinator selection, non-pollinator selection, genetic drift and gene flow as interacting explanations for flower-colour polymorphism and its geographic variation. Trunschke et al. (2021) further showed that direct evidence for pollinator-mediated selection on flower colour is more limited than is often assumed.

Abiotic and correlated-trait mechanisms are especially relevant for anthocyanin-based systems. Warren & Mackenzie (2001) found morph-by-water-treatment interactions across five polymorphic plant species: pigmented plants performed relatively better under drought whereas unpigmented plants performed relatively better under well-watered conditions. In *Linanthus parryae*, the relative fitness of blue and white morphs changed with annual precipitation, and reciprocal-transplant work linked spatial flower-colour differentiation to local environmental differences (Schemske & Bierzychudek 2001, 2007). In *Boechera stricta*, floral pigmentation was associated with herbivory, elevation and drought; drought induced pigmentation in some white-flowered lineages and white flowers had a fecundity advantage under well-watered conditions (Vaidya et al. 2018).

These studies justify treating moisture as a biologically plausible axis, but they do **not** imply that the present species-level moisture-breadth association is a direct anthocyanin stress-tolerance mechanism.

### 1.3 Flower colour and climate have already been studied at macroecological scales

The novelty cannot be “flower colour is related to climate.” Dalrymple et al. (2020), among others, related broad-scale flower-colour patterns to both biotic and abiotic conditions, including precipitation and radiation. Morph-level climatic niche differentiation and geographic colour-morph segregation have also been studied directly in individual animal species in Journal of Biogeography, including *Podarcis muralis* (Pérez i de Lanuza et al. 2018), *Varanus varius* (Farquhar et al. 2023), and *Vipera berus* (Jansen et al. 2025; Spaseni et al. 2025).

Those studies ask whether particular morphs or morph frequencies occupy different climates. The present study asks a different comparative question: **across species, is intraspecific colour variation organized locally or geographically under different species-level climatic niche contexts?**

### 1.4 Niche breadth and intraspecific variation are linked concepts, but the direction is not mechanistically unique

The niche variation hypothesis proposed that broader population niches can be associated with greater within-population phenotypic or ecological variation (Van Valen 1965). Modern plant ITV reviews emphasize that intraspecific variation affects niche breadth, performance, distributions and ecological interactions, while also stressing that the spatial structure of ITV remains insufficiently characterized (Westerband et al. 2021). At the species level, niche breadth is also positively related to geographic range size across many taxa and niche dimensions (Slatyer et al. 2013).

These literatures motivate a connection between climatic breadth and the spatial organization of variation, but they do not uniquely predict the causal route in the present study.

## 2. The actual gap

The directly defensible gap is:

> Previous work has examined mechanisms maintaining colour polymorphism within populations, geographic variation in morph frequencies, morph-specific climatic niches, and macroecological relationships between flower colour and climate. What has not been directly tested across species is whether **the spatial organization of intraspecific flower-colour variation itself** — local coexistence versus geographic differentiation — is associated with species-level occupied climatic niche breadth.

The response variable is therefore not flower colour, colour intensity, or the climate of an individual morph. It is a **biogeographic state of intraspecific phenotypic variation**.

## 3. Competing ecological expectations

The manuscript should avoid pretending that the observed direction was an inevitable prediction. The literature supports at least two reasonable expectations.

### H1. Broad-gradient environmental-sorting expectation

If geographically structured colour variation commonly reflects morph-specific sorting along broad climatic gradients, then species spanning a broad range of macroclimatic conditions should provide more opportunity for spatially varying selection and geographic replacement of morphs. Studies of colour morphs in lizards and snakes show that morph composition and morph-specific realised niches can be geographically associated with temperature, precipitation and aridity (Pérez i de Lanuza et al. 2018; Farquhar et al. 2023; Spaseni et al. 2025).

**Prediction:** geographically structured cases should show climatic breadth at least as great as, and potentially greater than, within-population cases.

This is an expectation from broad climatic sorting, not a universal theorem: geographic differentiation can also arise over narrow species-level climatic ranges through fine-scale selection, historical isolation or demographic structure.

### H2. Broad-niche coexistence / niche-variation expectation

The niche-variation tradition and plant ITV literature provide an alternative expectation: broad ecological occupancy can be associated with greater maintained intraspecific variation, while local balancing mechanisms such as negative frequency dependence, microhabitat variation or opposing biotic and abiotic selection can allow multiple phenotypes to coexist within populations (Van Valen 1965; Delph & Kelly 2014; Westerband et al. 2021).

**Prediction:** within-population polymorphic species should occupy broader climatic niches, whereas geographically structured cases should occur toward the narrower end of species-level occupied climatic breadth.

This prediction does not require broad niche breadth to *cause* coexistence. Broad climatic occupancy can instead be a macroecological correlate of ecological generalism, demographic persistence, or other unmeasured properties that make local maintenance of multiple phenotypes more likely.

## 4. What the frozen 34-species result says

The current analysis is directionally consistent with H2 rather than H1:

- all five climatic-niche odds ratios are below one;
- every leave-one-family-out estimate remains below one;
- Open Tree and dated V.PhyloMaker2 analyses retain the same negative direction;
- moisture breadth has the largest observed contrast;
- Holm adjustment, phylogenetic confidence intervals and CR2/Satterthwaite inference show substantial uncertainty.

The strongest defensible conclusion is therefore:

> Across the frozen literature-derived comparison, geographically structured flower-colour variation occurred toward the narrower end of sampled species-level climatic niche breadth than within-population coexistence. The shared direction across five climatic summaries is more important than any single threshold-significant result; moisture breadth is the strongest observed axis, not an established unique mechanism.

## 5. Ecological interpretation

### 5.1 What is directly supported

The result establishes an association between a species-level macroecological property (occupied climatic breadth) and the documented spatial organization of intraspecific flower-colour variation.

### 5.2 What is biologically interesting about the direction

A simple broad-gradient climatic-sorting explanation might have suggested that geographically differentiated species would span broad climatic conditions, allowing different morphs to track different climates. The observed pattern is the opposite: among-population cases tend to have narrower sampled climatic breadth.

Therefore geographic colour differentiation need not be interpreted as the endpoint of broad-scale climate sorting. Plausible alternatives include:

- fine-scale environmental differences that WorldClim does not resolve;
- spatially varying biotic interactions, including pollinators and herbivores;
- demographic history and founder effects;
- restricted dispersal or migration–selection balance;
- drift in subdivided populations;
- correlated traits or systemic anthocyanin functions.

These are **mechanistic hypotheses for future tests**, not variables measured by the present analysis.

### 5.3 Why moisture is worth discussing but not elevating to the whole claim

Moisture has unusually clear biological precedent in anthocyanin-based flower-colour systems: drought-dependent performance differences have been reported across multiple polymorphic species, *Linanthus parryae* shows precipitation-associated temporal and spatial selection, and *Boechera stricta* shows drought-linked pigmentation and fitness trade-offs (Warren & Mackenzie 2001; Schemske & Bierzychudek 2001, 2007; Vaidya et al. 2018).

The current data, however, contain species-level occupied climate and not morph-labelled occurrences. The paper should therefore say that moisture is a **priority axis for mechanistic follow-up**, not that drought generated geographic colour differentiation in the 34 species.

## 6. Novelty statement for the manuscript

Recommended wording:

> Previous studies have asked why particular colour morphs occur in particular environments. We instead ask a biogeographic question about the organization of intraspecific diversity itself: why are colour variants documented as coexisting within populations in some species but geographically differentiated in others, and is that spatial organization associated with species-level climatic niche breadth?

Short cover-letter version:

> We treat the spatial organization of intraspecific phenotypic variation — local coexistence versus geographic differentiation — as a comparative biogeographic trait, rather than treating flower colour itself as the response.

## 7. Journal decision

### 1st: Journal of Biogeography — Research Article

Best fit. JBI explicitly seeks research at the biology–geography intersection, asks authors to articulate theoretical foundations and conceptual advances, and welcomes comparative work across scales and taxa. Recent JBI articles directly demonstrate editorial interest in geographic variation of colour polymorphism and morph-specific climatic niches. The present paper adds a cross-species level of organization that those studies do not address.

The manuscript should therefore emphasize:

1. spatial organization of intraspecific diversity as the response trait;
2. competing theoretical expectations;
3. a cross-species comparative test rather than a flower-colour mechanism paper;
4. the counterintuitive direction relative to a simple broad-gradient sorting expectation;
5. explicit uncertainty and non-causal inference.

### 2nd: Ecography

Strong topical fit for spatial ecology and general ecological principles. However, the editorial bar for a general conceptual advance is higher. Ecography is most realistic if the paper is framed very explicitly as a general theory problem about spatial organization of ITV, with flower colour as the model trait rather than the central subject.

### 3rd: American Journal of Botany

Strong plant-biology fit and a sensible fallback. It can accommodate the flower-colour, abiotic-selection and plant-ITV story well, but it would make less use of the paper's distinctive biogeographic framing than JBI.

### Not first choice: Global Ecology and Biogeography

GEB explicitly prioritizes broad-scale patterns yielding globally general conceptual conclusions. The current study is broad geographically and taxonomically, but the frozen inferential sample is 34 species and the classification is literature-derived with acknowledged uncertainty. The paper can contribute a new comparative biogeographic pattern without yet claiming the level of general macroecological law that would make GEB the clearest first target.

## 8. Reviewer-facing limits that must remain explicit

- The 34 species are a literature-derived evidence set, not a random sample of angiosperms.
- Current spatial labels are source-traceable and rule-derived; independent blinded review is prepared but not yet completed.
- Species-level occupied climatic breadth is not physiological tolerance.
- GBIF occurrences are not labelled by flower-colour morph.
- The analysis is associative and does not directly estimate gene flow, demographic fragmentation, local selection or morph-specific climate.
- All five effect estimates share a direction, but multiplicity, phylogenetic and finite-cluster analyses limit threshold-significance claims.

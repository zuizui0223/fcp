# White-state heat × pigment-pathway intersection — 2026-09-25

## Status

Post-result mechanistic triangulation. This synthesis was assembled **after** the third-cohort BIO5, seasonal-heat, legacy-cohort replication, transition-direction and natural pigment-mechanism results were opened. It is therefore not a new confirmatory test and cannot rescue any failed environmental gate.

The purpose is narrower:

> Do experimentally demonstrated high-temperature effects on floral pigmentation perturb the same molecular nodes that recur in natural white/pigmented FCP systems?

## FCP natural white/pigmented molecular nodes

The natural-FCP manual audit identifies the following normalized node classes:

- **CHS** — *Ipomoea purpurea*, *Parrya nudicaulis*
- **F3H** — *Silene littorea*
- **ANS** — *Gymnadenia rhellicani*, *Pleione limprichtii*
- **UFGT** — *Pleione limprichtii*
- **FLS** — *Pleione limprichtii*
- **MYB** — *Gymnadenia rhellicani*, *Pleione limprichtii*, *Silene littorea*
- **bHLH** — *Ipomoea purpurea*, *Pleione limprichtii*
- **WD40** — *Pleione limprichtii*

This is an 8-node normalized FCP set. Evidence strength differs among species; the nodes summarize the verified mechanistic literature rather than claiming identical causal resolution.

## Independent floral heat-response evidence

### Antirrhinum majus

High temperature suppressed petal anthocyanin accumulation and reduced expression of **CHS, CHI, F3H, DFR, ANS and UFGT**. The effect was stronger under low light and was partly associated with reduced petal sugar status.

DOI: 10.1016/j.scienta.2021.110510

### Lilium spp.

At 35°C, susceptible bud stages showed reduced pigmentation together with suppression of **LhMYB12, CHS, F3H and DFR**.

DOI: 10.1016/j.scienta.2011.09.030

### Chrysanthemum morifolium

High temperature induced the repressor **CmMYB012**, which directly suppressed **CHS, DFR, ANS and UFGT** and reduced pink floral pigmentation.

DOI: 10.1038/s41438-021-00675-z

### Rosa 'Jaguar'

Transient heat reduced anthocyanin concentration and reduced **CHS** and **DFR** transcript levels by about half.

DOI: 10.1016/S0168-9452(02)00417-X

### Moricandia arvensis

Natural spring lilac flowers switch plastically to white summer flowers under elevated temperature. Anthocyanins were detected only in spring flowers; summer white flowers instead accumulated larger amounts of UV-absorbing flavonoids and other phenolics.

DOI: 10.1002/ajb2.70096

This species provides direct phenotype/biochemical evidence for heat-driven white floral plasticity, but not a gene-specific node for the overlap count below.

## Node-level intersection

Direct heat-responsive flower-pigmentation nodes in the selected primary literature:

`MYB, CHS, CHI, F3H, DFR, ANS, UFGT`

FCP natural-white nodes:

`MYB, CHS, F3H, ANS, UFGT, FLS, bHLH, WD40`

Explicit intersection:

`MYB, CHS, F3H, ANS, UFGT`

Thus **5 of the 8 normalized FCP white-state node classes** also have direct experimental support as heat-responsive nodes in floral anthocyanin regulation.

This fraction is descriptive. No enrichment p-value is reported because the anthocyanin-pathway universe and heat-study ascertainment are not exchangeable random samples: high temperature often represses multiple nodes across the pathway.

## Mechanistic interpretation

The strongest integrated model is not:

`high temperature universally causes white flowers`

and not:

`white flowers universally arise by one loss-of-function mutation`.

It is:

`
genetic/regulatory perturbation ┐
                               ├─> reduced / rerouted pigment-pathway flux -> achromatic or pale floral endpoint
thermal repression ------------┘
`

Different genetic lesions and environmental heat can perturb the **same biochemical network** and therefore converge on a similar low-chromatic endpoint.

This resolves an apparent tension in the FCP results:

- a recurrent white/nonwhite phenotype-space axis can be real;
- direct natural molecular systems can converge on the pigment network;
- heat can produce the same endpoint in some species;
- yet BIO5 does not need to replicate as a universal cross-species ecological predictor, because thermal sensitivity is genotype-, developmental-stage- and light-dependent.

## Why cohort-dependent BIO5 is compatible with this model

The primary heat literature itself shows strong conditionality:

- *Lilium*: only specific flower-bud stages were heat-sensitive;
- *Antirrhinum*: heat suppression was stronger under low light;
- chrysanthemum: heat-sensitive and heat-stable genotypes differ in response;
- *Moricandia*: seasonal white flowers are a reversible plastic phenotype.

Therefore a global photo-level BIO5 effect can appear in one species composition and disappear in another even if thermal repression of anthocyanin is a real molecular process.

The failed discovery/reserve BIO5 replication still rules out promoting heat to a universal FCP driver. It does **not** contradict pathway-level mechanistic compatibility.

## New working hypothesis

### Heat-sensitive pigment-network accessibility hypothesis

Species differ in how close their floral pigment network operates to a low-flux threshold. Genetic variants that reduce structural-gene activity or MBW regulation, and environments that repress the same pathway, can both move flowers toward the achromatic endpoint.

Predictions:

1. pigmented morphs from natural white/pigmented systems should lose anthocyanin disproportionately under heat when their causal/regulatory node is heat-responsive;
2. genotype × temperature interactions should be stronger than a universal temperature main effect;
3. heat-induced fading should preferentially reduce expression at the same node classes observed in natural white variants;
4. species with pathway lesions near early/bottleneck steps (e.g. CHS/F3H or upstream MYB control) may show stronger threshold-like colour loss than systems whose variation is mainly hue-routing downstream;
5. white morphs already near the pigment floor should show smaller further chromatic change, producing asymmetric reaction norms.

## Best decisive experiment

A common-garden factorial experiment is more diagnostic than another global BIO5 regression:

`
natural colour genotype/morph × temperature
`

with at least two temperatures and repeated measurements of:

- floral reflectance / CIELAB;
- total anthocyanin;
- flavonol/flavone allocation;
- targeted expression of CHS, F3H, DFR, ANS, UFGT and MYB/bHLH/WD40 regulators.

The key test is the **genotype/morph × temperature interaction**, not the temperature main effect.

## Claim ceiling

This synthesis supports:

> genetic and thermal perturbations can converge on overlapping anthocyanin/flavonoid network nodes and thereby provide a mechanistic route to recurrent achromatic floral phenotypes.

It does not establish that heat generated the global white/nonwhite H2 geometry, that all white flowers are anthocyanin-deficient, or that pigmented-to-white transitions are evolutionarily more frequent.

# RGFCA image measurement and ecological interpretation: literature audit

Audit date: 7 September 2026. Bounded primary-source reading for the active
RGFCA manuscript, not a systematic review or an analysis of new image outcomes.
This note changes no frozen cohort, measurement, statistical rule, or result.
No reserve image or reserve outcome file was opened.

## Scope and decision

The manuscript's discovery frame is 369 species and 21,424 photographs,
represented by uncalibrated four-component palette mixtures. The separately
reserved 500-species, 50,000-photo measurement is outside this literature audit.
These study facts are taken from the local [RGFCA manuscript](RGFCA_MANUSCRIPT.md),
not established by the external papers below.

Do not frame RGFCA as the first image-first flower-colour study, the first
large multispecies floral-image analysis, or a census of all flowering plants.
The defensible contribution to assess is its linked, species-conditioned
within-species/sharedness design and explicit falsification/replication
architecture. This is a positioning judgement, not a verified priority claim.

## Six directly relevant primary studies

### 1. Measurement validation is possible, but does not transfer automatically

**Reference:** Alexandra Laitly, Corey T. Callaghan, Kaspar Delhey, and William K.
Cornwell (2021). *Is color data from citizen science photographs reliable for
biodiversity research?* Ecology and Evolution 11:4071–4083.
DOI: [10.1002/ece3.7307](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.7307).

**Read/access:** Publisher full-text abstract, Methods 2.1–2.2 and Results;
[successfully opened Wiley text](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.7307).

**Supports:** The authors compared photograph-derived colour with controlled
measurements, including two flower species. Less controlled photography added
variation, and consumer photographs did not recover ultraviolet information.
Their often-cited 12–14-image averaging recommendation concerns **interspecific
bird comparisons**, not a universal within-species flower sampling threshold.
[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.7307).

**Does not support / RGFCA transfer:** This does not calibrate RGFCA's particular
palette, automated masks, or spatial effect. More photographs can reduce some
noise without eliminating systematic, geographically patterned error. Report
photo-derived visible-colour variation, not spectral reflectance, pollinator
perception, or validated botanical morphs.

### 2. Direct precedent for landscape flower-colour maps and calibration checks

**Reference:** Yvonne Luong, Ariel Gasca-Herrera, Tracy M. Misiewicz, and Benjamin
E. Carter (2023). *A pipeline for the rapid collection of color data from
photographs.* Applications in Plant Sciences 11(5):e11546.
DOI: [10.1002/aps3.11546](https://doi.org/10.1002/aps3.11546).

**Read/access:** Publisher full-text Methods, Results, and relevant Discussion,
including colour correction and colour-selection challenges;
[successfully opened publisher text](https://bsapubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/aps3.11546).

**Supports:** Approximately 4,800 iNaturalist *Erysimum* records were used for
geographical colour mapping, spatial tests, and climate comparisons. In a
separate field-photo check, calibrated and uncalibrated analyses recovered
qualitatively similar selected population contrasts. Pixel sampling was manual,
avoiding unsuitable petals/images; the authors discuss automated extraction
difficulties when flowers resemble backgrounds.
[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/aps3.11546).

**Does not support / RGFCA transfer:** Those checks do not validate automated
multispecies ROI extraction or RGFCA's small omnibus effect. Treat tissue
selection, colour representation, and downstream association as separate
validation targets. Spatial structure need not imply a strong universal climate
relationship or one ecological cause.

### 3. Direct precedent for high-throughput within-species floral phenotyping

**Reference:** Patrick F. McKenzie, Samuel H. Church, and Robin Hopkins (2026).
*High-Throughput iNaturalist Image Analysis Reveals Flower Color Divergence in
Monarda fistulosa.* The American Naturalist 208(1):101–109.
DOI: [10.1086/739413](https://doi.org/10.1086/739413).

**Read/access:** Final publisher abstract and bibliographic record were
[successfully opened](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/739413), with
[PubMed corroboration](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42313785/). Final full text
was marked **No Access**. The earlier author preprint,
[10.1101/2025.05.21.655392](https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.05.21.655392), was read
through indexed [PMC Methods and Validation analysis sections](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12154663/);
direct PMC opens encountered a browser check. Publisher and PubMed online dates
disagree; use the verified 2026 issue year, not an inferred online date.

**Supports:** The final abstract reports automated flower identification/colour
extraction from more than 40,000 photographs and darker, higher-chroma western
flowers. The **preprint Methods** describe trained flower/non-flower segmentation
and a CIELAB geometric median; those details are not independently checked
against the final full text here.
[Published abstract](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/739413),
[preprint Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12154663/).

**Does not support / RGFCA transfer:** A single-species pipeline and a regional
contrast do not validate all-species masks or identify shared global boundaries.
Do not import its validation performance into RGFCA. Its continuous perceptual
coordinates also differ from RGFCA's four grouped palette components.

### 4. Large multispecies image-derived colour and pollinator context already exist

**Reference:** Patrick F. McKenzie, Andrea E. Berardi, and Robin Hopkins (2025).
*Delayed flowering phenology of red-flowering plants in response to hummingbird
migration.* Current Biology 35(9):2175–2182.e3.
DOI: [10.1016/j.cub.2025.03.035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2025.03.035).

**Read/access:** The complete final abstract/bibliographic record was available
through indexed [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40233751/).
Published Results sections “Generation of a North American flower color database”,
“Patterns of flowering plants across North America”
and “Pollinator presence is associated with flower color phenology differences”
were available through indexed [PMC text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12062948/).
Direct publisher/PMC opens were unreliable. Selected STAR Methods passages on
observation filtering and pollinator association were also retrieved from the
indexed published article; final colour-assignment Methods were **not verified**.
The 2024 preprint's different title, *A massive community-science dataset reveals
convergent evolution of delayed flowering phenology in North American
red-flowering plants*, must not replace the published citation.

**Supports:** The published abstract reports colour classification spanning more
than 11,000 species and 1.6 million observations. Results relate delayed
red/orange flowering in eastern North America to hummingbird timing, with
bumblebees as a comparison. Thus image-derived multispecies colour geography and
pollinator context are established precedents.
[Published abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40233751/),
[published Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12062948/).

**Does not support / RGFCA transfer:** These findings do not validate RGFCA's
within-species estimand or establish a worldwide pollinator boundary. The
default-photo species-label method is visible in the earlier preprint, but must
be checked against final Methods before asserting that precise contrast in the
manuscript. Pollinator overlap is not direct visitation or fitness evidence.

### 5. Observer behaviour is part of the observation process

**Reference:** Grace J. Di Cecco, Vijay Barve, Michael W. Belitz, Brian J. Stucky,
Robert P. Guralnick, and Allen H. Hurlbert (2021). *Observing the Observers: How
Participants Contribute Data to iNaturalist and Implications for Biodiversity
Science.* BioScience 71(11):1179–1188.
DOI: [10.1093/biosci/biab093](https://doi.org/10.1093/biosci/biab093).

**Read/access:** Publisher abstract and opening substantive text were
[successfully opened](https://academic.oup.com/bioscience/article/71/11/1179/6357804?login=false).
No numerical results from unread later sections are used here.

**Supports:** The study examines iNaturalist observations from 2008–2019,
documents taxonomic specialization, and investigates observer activity. Opportunistic
records lack a fully specified sampling process, so their bias cannot be assumed
absent merely because coordinates and dates are present.
[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/biosci/biab093).

**Does not support / RGFCA transfer:** Observer caps and spatial balancing do not
turn RGFCA into probability sampling of all plants or locations. Excluding
same-observer pairs addresses one dependence route, not shared equipment,
accessible habitats, selective photography, or every regional bias. This paper
does not show that the current RGFCA effect is artefactual; it motivates explicit
checks and restricted population claims.

### 6. Phenology and pollinator context require matched biological resolution

**Reference:** Yingying Xie, Hanna T. Thammavong, and Daniel S. Park (2022).
*The ecological implications of intra- and inter-species variation in
phenological sensitivity.* New Phytologist 236(2):760–773.
DOI: [10.1111/nph.18361](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.18361).

**Read/access:** Publisher Summary and Methods, including image classification,
geographical/phenological distributions, and statistical modelling;
[successfully opened publisher text](https://nph.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/nph.18361).

**Supports:** This study jointly examined *Claytonia virginica* colour groups,
phenology, and the specialist bee *Andrena erigeniae*. Climate sensitivities
varied among regions and colour groups. Its Methods distinguish observed
phenological peaks from complete flowering seasons and acknowledge residual
spatiotemporal sampling bias despite regional random effects.
[Primary paper](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.18361).

**Does not support / RGFCA transfer:** A calendar quarter is not an observed
flowering stage, nor is a broad bee biogeographic zone an interaction network.
Any future RGFCA pollinator extension needs independently sourced identities,
seasonal overlap, and a defined response before outcome inspection. This
one-plant/specialist-bee system cannot establish universal bumblebee mediation.

## Targeted novelty-search coverage

Searches used an internet search index and followed candidate hits to publisher,
PubMed/PMC, or author-controlled primary resources. Key queries included:

1. `flower colour citizen science photographs iNaturalist colour quantification study`
2. `global flower colour mapping image photographs multispecies iNaturalist`
3. `"flower colour" "global" "iNaturalist" "atlas"`
4. Exact-title follow-ups for the *Erysimum*, *Monarda*, and North American
   colour/phenology studies, including checks for published versions of preprints.

The search was English-language, targeted, and bounded. It did not exhaust
subscription databases, all preprints, non-English work, or every reference
network. Search hits outside these six studies are leads, not audited evidence.
Failure to find an exact RGFCA counterpart is **not evidence of absence**.

The immediate manuscript action is to acknowledge the verified precedents and
state the particular estimand being tested, not claim priority. A priority claim
would require a wider documented review. Literature-supported plausibility also
cannot substitute for the pending RGFCA measurement and replication gates.

## Operational boundaries for using this note

- Keep exploratory discovery, independent replication, and mechanism attribution
  separate. Neither a prior positive paper nor a green software check establishes
  the current study's ecological validity.
- Preserve the fixed ongoing analysis. These readings are interpretation and
  future-design inputs, not permission to retune the frozen reserve analysis.
- Do not label uncalibrated palette mixtures as pigment concentration, genetic
  colour morphs, or pollinator visual distances.
- Before submission, recheck final-version bibliographic fields and obtain the
  unread final Methods wherever an exact methodological comparison is retained.
  The access ceilings above are part of the audit, not missing text to infer.

No verbatim passages are reproduced. Each study's substantive summary is kept
short to leave room for a brief, separately cited manuscript statement.

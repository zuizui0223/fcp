# Floral-specific pigment decoupling hypothesis — 2026-09-25

## Core hypothesis

The recurrent accessibility of white/achromatic floral phenotypes is facilitated not merely by pigment loss, but by **regulatory decoupling of floral anthocyanin production from flavonoid functions required elsewhere in the plant**.

The strongest formulation is:

> White floral phenotypes should be evolutionarily and ecologically easier to maintain when reduced pigmentation is restricted to petals or to the floral anthocyanin branch, thereby preserving vegetative and/or non-anthocyanin flavonoid functions that protect against abiotic stress.

This is a post-result mechanistic hypothesis generated from the FCP white-axis result and subsequent literature/public-data audits. It is not a confirmatory result.

## Evidence anchors

### Parrya nudicaulis — direct tissue-specific decoupling

Natural purple-white polymorphism across Alaska.

- white-flower frequency increases with growing-season temperature;
- white petals show an early anthocyanin-pathway block;
- CHS expression is ~24-fold lower in white petals;
- comparable CHS reduction is not observed in leaves or sepals;
- the study explicitly interprets this as decoupling petal and vegetative CHS expression.

This is the clearest natural example of floral-specific pigment loss with preserved extra-floral pathway expression.

DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0018230.

### Ipomoea purpurea A locus — systemic structural loss and abiotic cost

The rare white `a` allele disrupts CHS-D, an early structural anthocyanin/flavonoid-pathway gene.

- the white phenotype is associated with loss of pigmentation in flowers and most vegetative tissues;
- at high temperature, homozygous white `aa` plants have lower male and female fertilization success than pigmented `AA` plants;
- this genotype difference is not observed at low temperature.

Thus systemic loss of an early flavonoid-pathway step carries a measurable heat-stress cost.

DOI: 10.1046/j.1365-294X.2003.01786.x.

### Ipomoea purpurea W locus — regulatory white phenotype with lower apparent pleiotropic burden

A second natural white-flower polymorphism is caused by a nonfunctional R2R3-MYB regulator, IpMYB1.

- the white `w` allele can reach much higher natural frequencies than the structural CHS-null `a` allele;
- reviews of the system report no established deleterious pleiotropic fitness effect comparable to the A-locus structural null;
- flower buds show broad downregulation of anthocyanin structural genes.

This contrast is consistent with, but does not by itself prove, the prediction that regulatory changes can generate floral depigmentation with lower whole-plant pleiotropic cost.

Relevant DOI: 10.1266/ggs.73.105; IpMYB1 genetic evidence summarized in the Ipomoea evo-devo literature.

### Moricandia arvensis — environmentally induced branch decoupling

Within the same individuals:

- hotter summer treatment under the same stated summer photoperiod reduces cyanidin by ~72%;
- the kaempferol-equivalent flavonol measure is nearly unchanged at the cohort-mean level;
- summer-white flowers downregulate MYB90, CHS, U75C1/U78D2 and TTG1, plus PAL/4CL/DFR;
- returning plants toward spring conditions restores anthocyanin;
- the published phenotype changes from lilac/UV-reflecting to white/UV-absorbing.

The frozen log-ratio branch-specificity statistic was not estimable because of zero cyanidin values, so this remains mechanistic triangulation rather than confirmatory proof of flux rerouting.

DOI: 10.1038/s41467-020-17875-1.

## Why this can explain the FCP white/nonwhite axis

An anthocyanin/flavonoid network offers many routes to lower floral chromatic output:

- cis-regulatory reduction of a structural gene;
- loss or alteration of a floral MYB/bHLH/WD40 regulator;
- reduced expression at structural nodes;
- environmentally induced repression;
- redistribution toward non-anthocyanin flavonoids.

But mutations that globally disable an early pathway step can have large pleiotropic costs because flavonoids also contribute to stress physiology, reproduction and other tissues.

Therefore the accessible evolutionary target is not simply:

`pigment loss`

but more specifically:

`floral / anthocyanin-branch-specific pigment reduction with preserved nonfloral function`.

This is a many-to-one developmental architecture that can generate recurrent white/nonwhite geometry without requiring a universal ecological driver.

## Predictions

### D1 — regulatory/tissue-specific enrichment

Among naturally common white/pigmented polymorphisms with resolved molecular mechanisms, white states should more often involve:
- cis-regulatory changes;
- MYB/bHLH/WD40 regulators;
- flower-specific expression changes;

than globally expressed early-pathway structural nulls.

### D2 — pleiotropic-cost gradient

Systemic early-pathway nulls should show stronger abiotic/reproductive fitness costs and lower natural allele frequencies than flower-restricted regulatory white alleles, after controlling for demographic history and ascertainment.

### D3 — environment × genotype interaction

Temperature, drought, UV and other stresses should have larger fitness effects on systemic flavonoid-loss genotypes than on flower-specific white genotypes.

### D4 — plastic white phenotypes preserve alternative flavonoids

Environmentally induced white flowers should show a larger reduction in anthocyanins than in non-anthocyanin flavonols or vegetative flavonoids.

### D5 — tissue-expression test

For natural white/pigmented pairs, expression differences at causal/candidate nodes should be stronger in petals than in leaves/sepals when the white allele is common and persistent.

## Connection to current negative results

This hypothesis predicts that a universal BIO5 main effect may fail.

Temperature can:
- induce white flowers in systems where heat represses floral anthocyanin while alternative flavonoid functions remain;
- favor pigmented genotypes when pigment loss removes stress-protective flavonoids systemically;
- show weak or opposite effects depending on tissue specificity, developmental stage and pathway position.

Thus cross-species sign heterogeneity is expected under a shared pigment-network architecture.

## Claim ceiling

Current evidence supports this as a coherent working hypothesis with strong case-study anchors.

It does not yet establish:
- a statistically enriched prevalence of tissue-specific regulatory changes among all natural white polymorphisms;
- that regulatory white alleles are universally fitter than structural white alleles;
- that this mechanism alone explains the global FCP H2 geometry.

The decisive comparative test requires a curated molecular panel coding:
1. pathway node;
2. structural versus regulatory lesion;
3. tissue specificity;
4. natural allele/morph frequency;
5. abiotic fitness effect;
6. pigment class.

## Recommended first shot

### 1. New Phytologist — Full Paper

**Recommended first submission.**

Current journal fit:

- New Phytologist publishes novel, rigorous and timely plant science across Physiology & Development, Environment, Interaction, Evolution and Transformative Plant Biotechnology.
- Its Evolution scope includes phylogenetics, population/quantitative genetics, speciation and evo-devo, while explicitly favoring conceptual, question-driven work over narrowly taxon-specific studies.
- Full Papers are usually approximately 6,500–7,500 words with 6–8 display items.
- Initial submission uses a 200-word four-bullet Summary and free-format manuscript submission.

### Why the present paper fits

The study is:

- plant-wide rather than taxon-specific;
- question-driven;
- built around a species-level phenotype rather than a regional pattern alone;
- explicitly replicated/validated across observer-disjoint and species-disjoint designs;
- strongest on a plant-specific biological regularity: recurrent white-versus-nonwhite polymorphism geometry;
- accompanied by construction-preserving nulls and an untouched prospective confirmation.

The third-cohort prospective result materially improves the fit because the named axis is no longer only a post-audit pattern.

### Main editorial risk

The study identifies a recurrent geometric regularity but does not identify a biochemical, developmental or selective mechanism.

The manuscript should therefore sell the advance as:

> a general plant phenotypic regularity and a reproducible framework for macro-scale intraspecific trait geometry,

not as a mechanism paper.

### Article type

Prefer **Full Paper**, not Priority Report.

Priority Reports require a highly novel, time-sensitive case for fast-track publication and a shorter 4,500-word ceiling. The present study is novel but does not need a time-sensitive priority claim.

## Ambitious alternative

### 2. Evolution Letters — Letter

Current journal fit:

- Evolution Letters publishes cutting-edge evolutionary biology expected to substantially advance the field, show outstanding originality/clarity, or have broad interest.
- Typical Letters are about 5,000 words, excluding display items.

### Strength

The paper's strongest Evolution Letters argument is not “flower colour is variable.” It is:

> repeated within-species phenotypic variation across many lineages is constrained along one recurrent achromatic–chromatic geometry, and that already identified direction survives a prospective species-disjoint confirmation.

The prospective chronology and frozen target substantially strengthen originality.

### Editorial risk

The current paper does not directly reconstruct evolutionary transitions, identify selection, estimate repeated gains/losses, or show broad phylogenetic signal.

A desk editor may therefore regard the paper as large-scale trait measurement/macroecology rather than a direct evolutionary-process advance.

### Use

Keep as an ambitious alternative if the final Introduction/Discussion can make the evolutionary question explicit without exceeding the evidence.

Do not manufacture an evolutionary-direction claim to fit the journal.

## Broad conceptual alternative

### 3. The American Naturalist — Article

Current journal mission emphasizes conceptual integration across evolution, ecology and behavior.

### Strength

The paper has a clean conceptual sequence:

- measurement validity;
- recurrent trait geometry;
- prospective confirmation;
- negative broad explanatory tests.

The combination of macro-scale natural-history data and a general conceptual question is compatible with the journal.

### Risk

The current manuscript is plant-centred and method/data-heavy; it would need a slightly stronger general argument about how species-level trait distributions can be measured and compared across organisms.

## Lower fit despite high visibility

### 4. Ecology Letters — Letter

Ecology Letters prioritizes highly novel ecological hypothesis tests and discourages work that is mainly descriptive or that merely extends prior results.

The current paper is strongly hypothesis-driven and prospectively confirmed, but the positive H2 result is geometric rather than an ecological mechanism or environmental driver.

The H3 ecological/geographic tests are negative.

Therefore the current form is less naturally ecological than New Phytologist or American Naturalist.

---

## Recommended submission sequence

1. **New Phytologist — Full Paper**
2. **Evolution Letters — Letter**, if the evolutionary framing remains defensible at submission
3. **The American Naturalist**
4. **Journal of Ecology / Evolution** as later strong-field alternatives depending on editor feedback

Do not run new H3 predictor searches merely to improve journal fit.

## New Phytologist adaptation checklist

The canonical manuscript should remain unchanged as the evidence-grounded source.

Create a journal-specific derivative with:

1. title under ~130 characters;
2. four-bullet Summary, <=200 words;
3. 5–8 alphabetical keywords;
4. sections:
   - Summary
   - Introduction
   - Materials and Methods
   - Results
   - Discussion
   - Acknowledgements
   - Competing interests
   - Author contributions
   - Data availability
   - References
   - Supporting Information legends;
5. continuous line numbering only at final typesetting/submission stage;
6. front-page word counts and display-item counts;
7. current five main figures plus either one concise table/display or an editor-acceptable five-figure presentation;
8. Discussion kept <=30% of main text where practicable.

## Cover-letter framing

The cover letter should answer New Phytologist's three presubmission-style questions directly:

### What question?

Can within-species flower-colour polymorphism be measured as a reproducible species-level phenotype, and does it repeatedly occupy a constrained direction in colour space?

### Advance

The paper moves from:
- observer-disjoint measurement validation,
to
- target discovery/audit,
to
- untouched species-disjoint prospective confirmation of the frozen white/nonwhite axis.

### Importance/timeliness

The study demonstrates how high-volume community-science imagery can be used to study within-species trait distributions across hundreds of plant species while separating measurement validation, target discovery and prospective confirmation.

## Claim boundary during journal adaptation

Journal framing must not change the frozen scientific ceiling.

In particular, do not add claims of:
- global prevalence;
- independent-source replication;
- anthocyanin loss/gain mechanism;
- evolutionary direction;
- pollinator/climate adaptation;
- broad phylogenetic absence.

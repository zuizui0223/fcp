# Floral-specific pigment decoupling: natural-frequency evidence — 2026-09-25

## Within-species evidence: Silene littorea

Public Supplementary Table S2 was parsed directly from the original DOCX.

Across 21 natural populations:

### PAL — petal anthocyanin loss
- positive in 2 populations;
- 6 positive population-year observations;
- positive frequency range: 8–21%;
- positive-frequency median: 15.5%;
- both PAL populations were positive in every repeated survey year available:
  - Louro: 21%, 9%, 8% in 2013/2014/2016;
  - Barra: 20%, 14%, 17% in 2013/2014/2016.

### WAL — whole-plant anthocyanin loss
- positive in 9 populations;
- 17 positive population-year observations;
- positive frequency range: 0.05–0.86%;
- positive-frequency median: 0.21%.

### Descriptive contrast
- PAL/WAL positive-frequency median ratio: 73.8×;
- minimum positive PAL frequency: 8%;
- maximum positive WAL frequency: 0.86%;
- every positive PAL observation exceeds every positive WAL observation.

Published tissue chemistry verifies that this is genuinely a tissue-scope contrast:
- PAL and WAL both have near-zero petal anthocyanin;
- PAL retains anthocyanin in calyx/stem photosynthetic tissues;
- WAL lacks anthocyanin broadly in those tissues;
- flavones remain abundant in both white phenotypes.

This is strong within-species natural support for the idea that petal-restricted pigment loss can persist at much higher frequency than whole-plant pigment loss.

## Cross-species literature evidence

Supplementary Table S1 from the same study contains:
- 13 PAL systems across 8 source-listed taxonomic groups;
- 13 WAL systems across 9 source-listed groups.

To avoid post hoc midpoint imputation, frequencies were retained as exact values, ranges, upper bounds, or qualitative descriptors.

### PAL
- median lower bound: 5%;
- median upper bound: 43%;
- minimum upper endpoint: 10%;
- maximum upper endpoint: 100%;
- 7/13 systems have lower bounds greater than 1.4%.

### WAL
Among natural non-greenhouse systems:
- 8 have numeric exact/upper-bound frequencies;
- median numeric upper bound: 0.1%;
- largest numeric upper bound: 1.4%;
- 3 additional systems are described as “rare”;
- 1 is described as “extremely rare”;
- 1 greenhouse frequency was excluded from the natural numeric panel.

### Conservative contrast
- PAL median lower bound / WAL median upper bound = 50×;
- minimum PAL upper endpoint / maximum quantified WAL upper bound = 7.14×;
- every PAL upper endpoint exceeds the maximum quantified WAL upper bound.

This is literature-assembled descriptive evidence, not an unbiased angiosperm-wide meta-analysis.

## Interpretation

The same qualitative pattern appears at two scales:

1. within one species with a direct tissue comparison;
2. across a literature-assembled set of unrelated species.

The pattern is consistent with a persistence filter:

`
petal / floral anthocyanin loss
        >
whole-plant anthocyanin loss
`

in attainable natural frequency.

Combined with molecular case studies, the mechanism hypothesis is:

> recurrent white floral phenotypes are especially evolvable when pigment reduction is decoupled from extra-floral flavonoid functions.

## Remaining limitation

A strict molecular-architecture × pleiotropic-cost comparison remains blocked by coverage:
- strict DEC mechanisms = 3;
- strict SYS mechanisms = 3;
- frozen requirement = 5 per class.

Therefore the current evidence supports a strong mechanistic pattern, but not yet a family-aware comparative test proving that regulatory/tissue-specific mutations systematically incur lower pleiotropic cost than systemic structural nulls.

## Hard nonclaims
- tissue restriction is associated with, not proven to cause, higher natural frequency;
- source systems are literature-ascertained;
- mutation rate, demography and other selection can contribute;
- this does not alter the frozen New Phytologist manuscript.

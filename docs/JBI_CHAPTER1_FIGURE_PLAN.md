# JBI Chapter 1 — canonical spatial figure plan

This figure set is separate from the existing 34-species comparative manuscript figures in `docs/FIGURE_PLAN.md`. It is generated only from the frozen 720-photo evaluation and its completed Stage A/B products.

**Target set:** four main-text figures plus two Supporting Figures.

## Selection rule

Every main figure answers one reader question:

1. **Is within-species continuous flower colour spatially organized?**
2. **Is the global Stage-A result broadly shared or heterogeneous among species?**
3. **Where was cross-species transition overlap actually evaluable, and what did the observed surface look like?**
4. **Is the Stage-B conclusion robust across the predeclared spatial supports?**

The figures do not search for environmental explanations because the frozen Stage-B primary gate was not passed.

## Figure C1 — Stage-A confirmatory null and graph-scale stability

### Panel A

Histogram/density of the 9,999 primary (`k = 5`) equal-species null statistics, with the observed statistic and null mean marked.

### Panel B

Standardized clustering deficit for `k = 3`, `k = 5` and `k = 8`, with lower-tail Monte Carlo p-values printed beside the points.

### Purpose

Show directly that the primary null was rejected and that the direction is not an artefact of the chosen nearest-neighbour degree.

### Interpretive boundary

A positive clustering deficit means lower observed edge discontinuity than expected. It does not identify the cause of spatial organization.

## Figure C2 — Species heterogeneity in Stage A

Horizontal dot plot of the species-specific standardized clustering deficits in the primary `k = 5` graph. Point size encodes graph edge count; labels report the frozen species-level lower-tail p-values.

### Purpose

Expose the six species behind the equal-species global statistic. The figure should make clear that *Lysimachia arvensis* and *Raphanus sativus* provide the strongest primary support while the global inference does not require every species to be individually significant.

### Interpretive boundary

Species-level p-values are descriptive components of a prospectively defined global test, not six newly selected primary hypotheses.

## Figure C3 — Primary shared-transition surface and opportunity

Global map of the primary 500-km/36×18 equal-area support:

- grey points: the 720 frozen evaluation locations;
- coloured cells: observed shared transition intensity `S(x)` for cells with `A(x) ≥ 2`;
- cell marker size: opportunity denominator `A(x)`;
- non-evaluable cells remain absent, not zero.

A compact inset/bar panel reports the number of cells with `A ≥ 2`, `A ≥ 3` and `A ≥ 4` and the per-species detectable-cell counts.

### Purpose

Distinguish observed intensity from detectability and show why the shared surface covers only a subset of global space.

### Interpretive boundary

The map is the observed frozen surface. Its strongest cells are not independently significant local discoveries, and no environmental overlay is shown.

## Figure C4 — Stage-B scale sensitivity

Plot the upper-tail Monte Carlo p-value for all nine predeclared edge-cap/grid combinations. The selected primary 500-km/36×18 configuration is marked distinctly; a horizontal line shows 0.05.

A second panel shows standardized concentration excess for the same configurations.

### Purpose

Make the inferential asymmetry explicit: the primary p-value is 0.0906, one coarser sensitivity configuration is nominally below 0.05, and the remaining supports are not. This is scale-dependent exploratory overlap, not a robust universal boundary.

## Supporting Figure C-S1 — Frozen workflow and gates

Schematic of:

`1,200 acquired → 480 calibration / 720 evaluation → continuous representation frozen → Stage A gate passed → Stage B primary gate not passed → geographic correspondence not promoted`.

### Purpose

Make the order of freezing, opening and inference auditable in one page.

## Supporting Figure C-S2 — Stage-B detectability matrix

Heatmap/matrix for the primary configuration showing, for each species and each globally evaluable cell:

- whether the species was detectable;
- its transition intensity when detectable;
- the cell opportunity denominator.

Cells are ordered geographically rather than by observed intensity.

### Purpose

Show the denominator behind the shared surface and prevent a visually strong cell from being mistaken for six-species agreement when only two or three species were evaluable.

## Canonical legends

### Figure C1

**Figure C1. Species-conditioned spatial organization of continuous flower colour.** (A) The observed equal-species mean edge discontinuity in the primary five-nearest-neighbour graph (vertical line) relative to 9,999 null values generated by permuting complete standardized colour vectors strictly within species while holding observation geometry fixed. Lower values indicate stronger local colour similarity. (B) Standardized clustering deficit across the primary and predeclared graph-degree sensitivities. All three lower-tail tests reject random labelling at 0.05.

### Figure C2

**Figure C2. Heterogeneous species contributions to the global Stage-A result.** Points show species-specific standardized clustering deficits in the primary five-nearest-neighbour graph; larger positive values indicate lower observed discontinuity than the species-conditioned null. Point size represents retained graph-edge count. The prospectively defined global test gives each species equal weight; individual p-values are shown to describe heterogeneity rather than to redefine the global hypothesis.

### Figure C3

**Figure C3. Observed shared-transition intensity under the frozen primary spatial support.** Grey points are the 720 held-out evaluation locations. Coloured cells show mean ranked transition intensity among detectable species for the primary 500-km edge cap and 36×18 equal-area grid. Marker size represents the opportunity denominator `A`, the number of species with sufficient geometry support in the cell. Cells with fewer than two detectable species are not evaluable and are not plotted as zero.

### Figure C4

**Figure C4. Shared-transition concentration is sensitive to spatial support and is not confirmed by the primary test.** Upper-tail Monte Carlo p-values and standardized concentration excesses are shown for the selected primary configuration and all predeclared passing sensitivity configurations. The primary 500-km/36×18 analysis did not reject the shared-concentration null (`p = 0.0906`). One coarser 500-km sensitivity was nominally below 0.05, whereas the remaining configurations were not, so the shared-boundary claim is not promoted.

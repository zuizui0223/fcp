### Benefits and costs that are *real* in biological studies

**A. Abiotic/vegetative protection versus reproductive output, plus plasticity — Boechera stricta.** Vaidya et al. (2018, New Phytologist, DOI 10.1111/nph.14998) compared wild, common-garden and glasshouse plants. Purple-flowered forms experienced less leaf damage, floral pigmentation could be induced by drought, and in well-watered conditions white forms had a fecundity advantage (glasshouse treatment). The whole-plant correlated phenotype and water-dependent benefit mean the direct cause need not be pigment synthesis energy. Do NOT generalize this fitness crossover to arbitrary FCP photographic white labels.

**B. Restriction of pleiotropic loss to petals — Silene littorea.** Del Valle et al. (2019, BMC Plant Biology, DOI 10.1186/s12870-019-2082-6) distinguished (i) flowers that lost anthocyanin only in petals (PAL) but retained photosynthetic-tissue anthocyanins and (ii) whole-plant loss (WAL). The PAL morph maintained 8–21% in two polymorphic populations, whereas WAL remained <1%. This offers a genuine **organ-specific cost-of-loss / developmental-constraint** candidate, but published 13 PAL vs 13 WAL cross-systems are biased and have only 2+2 FCP image overlaps. The current photo data cannot label PAL vs WAL. This result does NOT independently measure the metabolic resource savings of pale petals.

**C. Antagonists versus display / fitness — Abronia fragrans.** Jaeger et al. (AJB, published online 2025, issue 2026, PMID 41408544) found less-pigmented morphs made more flowers, whereas that did NOT translate into a reproductive advantage; florivory, foliar herbivory and seed set varied among sites, and pigment heritability was measured. This specifically cautions that even a phenotypic display–pigmentation cost correlation need not become a fitness tradeoff.

**D. Pollinator service versus florivory — Anemone coronaria.** Saabna et al. (Plant Biology, PMID 39607790) reported morph-specific florivore damages and beetle interactions and considered a service–damage balance. A hypothesized balancing component is not automatically direct evidence of stable white and coloured alleles at one mating population.

### Why the two-step question matters

If a pigment function has a fixed net selection advantage in every locality, a single form tends toward dominance rather than stable coexistence (subject to mutation, migration and drift). Range-wide persistence of visible alternatives can instead arise when the sign of net benefit minus cost varies across geographic contexts, a frequency-dependent advantage changes with local morph frequency, temporally variable selection interacts with seed banks, or recurrent mutations/plasticity replenish states. The same range-wide photo mosaic is also compatible with neutral spatial drift, restricted propagule dispersal and observation confounding.

A qualitative bookkeeping model is

    relative-coloured-fitness(environment, frequency)
      = protection + pollinator-service + other correlated-phenotype benefits
        - reproductive/display resources - consumer costs - pleiotropic costs.

Every term may vary by lineage and ecosystem, and several terms need not be present. Merely asserting a cost and benefit does not prove an equilibrium; competing components must be measured in the same reproductive-fitness currency.

### Ranked next empirical evidence required

1. **Cross-species biochemical white-state validation (most diagnostic for white-specific constraint):** sample petals and vegetative tissues, quantify anthocyanins plus protective flavonoids, classify PAL, WAL and non-anthocyanin white/yellow variants, including exposure-calibrated reference images. This separates true pigment loss from camera whitening.
2. **Within-population frequency and lifetime fitness:** real wild-morph/genotype frequencies, flower number, fruit/viable seed production, survival, outcrossing/selfing, and herbivore/florivore/pollinator rates; do not rely on species-wide iNaturalist photographs as a population census.
3. **Across-context sign crossover:** within matched genotypes/lineages replicate stressful versus benign site treatments and pollinator/antagonist manipulations; test whether the *net fitness* difference between pigmented and white forms changes sign, accounting for seed bank/gene flow and lineage relatedness.

The most appealing general biological question remains:

> When can a plant lose **floral** pigmentation without losing **whole-plant** protective functions, and does this reduce the evolutionary cost sufficiently to allow white and chromatic forms to recur and persist across different places?

This is falsifiable and biologically important, but the current three photo cohorts cannot demonstrate it. The global FCP evidence is strong for repeated *phenotypic* axes and geographic assortment, not for universal balancing selection driven by floral pigment resource costs.

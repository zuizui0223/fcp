# Mutation supply versus evolutionary filtering in recurrent white floral phenotypes

Date: 2026-09-25

## Purpose

This note connects the FCP floral-specific pigment-decoupling results to the broader flower-colour evo-devo literature.

The key question is:

> Are recurrent white floral phenotypes generated because regulatory white mutations arise more often, or because many mutation types arise but only low-pleiotropy/tissue-restricted variants persist and reach appreciable frequency?

## Published mutation-spectrum evidence

Wessinger & Rausher (2012), synthesizing Streisfeld & Rausher and related work, report that genetically characterized spontaneous white-flower mutants span both structural and regulatory classes:

- 29 / 69: functional mutations in core pathway enzymes;
- 8 / 69: cis-regulatory mutations affecting core pathway enzymes;
- 32 / 69: transcription-factor mutations.

Thus spontaneous white mutations are not restricted to one molecular class.

The same review reports that characterized evolutionary white-flower losses that passed stronger evolutionary filters are heavily biased toward anthocyanin transcriptional regulators, especially R2R3-MYBs.

This published contrast supports an **evolutionary filtering** interpretation rather than a simple regulatory mutation-supply explanation.

## Mechanistic reason for the filter

Anthocyanin/flavonoid pathway architecture predicts different pleiotropic burdens.

### Early structural lesions
Coding loss-of-function in CHS, DFR or other pathway enzymes can:
- alter flux beyond flower colour;
- affect vegetative flavonoids and/or pigmentation;
- change stress-protective chemistry;
- impose reproductive or physiological costs.

### Floral regulators / cis-regulatory changes
R2R3-MYB paralogs and cis-regulatory changes can have narrow spatial expression domains.

They can therefore:
- reduce floral anthocyanin;
- preserve pigment or flavonoid functions elsewhere;
- produce a white floral endpoint with less whole-plant biochemical disruption.

This is the molecular form of floral-specific pigment decoupling.

## New FCP evidence consistent with the filter

### Silene littorea within species
Public Table S2 reanalysis:
- PAL: petal-only anthocyanin loss, 8–21%, repeatedly maintained in the same two populations;
- WAL: whole-plant anthocyanin loss, 0.05–0.86% whenever detected.

PAL positive-frequency median is 15.5% versus 0.21% for WAL.

### Cross-species PAL/WAL source synthesis
Public Table S1 reanalysis:
- 13 PAL systems: minimum documented maximum frequency 10%, median 43%;
- 13 WAL systems: every numeric upper bound <=1.4%, median upper bound 0.1%; four additional systems are rare/extremely rare.

Published-only/non-greenhouse sensitivity:
- 11 PAL and 11 WAL systems;
- the same 10% versus 1.4% conservative separation remains.

### Ipomoea purpurea within species
Two white-flower loci produce a similar floral endpoint:

- W locus / IpMYB1 regulatory white: common natural polymorphism; no comparable established deleterious pleiotropic burden.
- A locus / CHS-D structural white: exceptionally rare; high-temperature experiments show reduced male and female fertilization success of aa versus AA plants.

This is a direct same-species example of architecture, pleiotropy and frequency covarying in the predicted direction.

### Parrya nudicaulis
White flowers are associated with strong petal-specific CHS downregulation while leaves/sepals do not show a comparable reduction.

This is direct tissue-specific decoupling at an early pathway node.

## Revised causal architecture

The current working model is:

`
broad mutation supply
      ->
many possible depigmentation mechanisms
      ->
tissue/pathway architecture
      ->
pleiotropic burden
      ->
ecological performance
      ->
persistence / frequency / fixation
`

The recurrent white/nonwhite phenotype-space axis can therefore emerge even if:
- white mutations arise through many genes;
- ecological selection differs among species;
- temperature effects differ in sign;
- there is no universal geographic boundary.

The generality lies in the **accessible low-pleiotropy route to an achromatic floral endpoint**, not in a universal ecological driver.

## Relation to temperature

Temperature is one possible selective/physiological filter rather than the universal cause.

Evidence:
- Moricandia: hotter summer directly suppresses cyanidin and produces the white floral state through overlapping pigment-network nodes.
- Ipomoea A locus: systemic CHS loss produces a high-temperature reproductive cost.
- Parrya: white frequency covaries positively with growing-season temperature despite petal-specific pathway reduction.
- Ophiorrhiza and other systems can show the opposite ecological sign.

Therefore:

`
temperature x pigment architecture
`

is a more defensible general mechanism than:

`
temperature -> white
`.

## Evidence hierarchy

Strong:
- white mutations can arise through multiple molecular classes;
- petal-restricted versus whole-plant pigment loss has sharply different natural frequency patterns in the published PAL/WAL synthesis;
- two independent within-species systems (Silene, Ipomoea) fit the low-pleiotropy persistence prediction.

Moderate:
- floral-specific decoupling is a major cause of the cross-species frequency difference.

Not yet established:
- a formal causal-molecular meta-analysis across enough independent natural systems;
- an unbiased global effect size;
- the exact fraction of recurrent FCP white-axis geometry explained by this mechanism.

## Claim ceiling

The strongest current claim is:

> recurrent achromatic floral phenotypes are consistent with a broad mutation supply followed by strong evolutionary filtering for molecular architectures that reduce floral pigmentation while limiting extra-floral pleiotropic costs.

This should not be rewritten as:
- regulatory mutations are intrinsically more common;
- all white flowers are adaptive;
- temperature universally selects for white;
- white is always evolutionarily derived.

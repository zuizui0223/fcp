## 1. Intraspecific trait variation is a legitimate ecological object, not only noise around species means

**Palacio, F. X., Graco-Roza, C., de Bello, F., & Carmona, C. P. (2025).**
*Integrating intraspecific trait variability in functional diversity: An overview of methods and a guide for ecologists.*
Ecological Monographs 95(2): e70024.
DOI: 10.1002/ecm.70024.

### Supports

The review explicitly treats species as distributions of individual trait values rather than homogeneous units and surveys methods for incorporating intraspecific trait variation into functional-diversity analyses.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use to motivate the conceptual move from a single representative species colour toward a species-level distribution/within-species diversity phenotype.

### Does not support

It does not validate the present flower-colour measurement model, D estimator, observer-split rule or any current numerical result.

---

## 2. Within-population flower-colour variation is an established evolutionary problem with multiple possible maintaining processes

**Sapir, Y., Gallagher, M. K., & Senden, E. (2021).**
*What Maintains Flower Colour Variation within Populations?*
Trends in Ecology & Evolution 36(6): 507–519.
DOI: 10.1016/j.tree.2021.01.011.

### Supports

The review frames within-population flower-colour variation as a useful system for studying how phenotypic variation is created and maintained. It discusses multiple candidate processes rather than a single universal mechanism.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use in the Introduction to establish why flower-colour polymorphism is biologically informative, and in the Discussion to justify keeping pollinator, abiotic, genetic and neutral mechanisms open.

### Does not support

It does not imply that the present white/nonwhite axis is adaptive or pollinator-mediated.

---

## 3. Flower-colour polymorphism can combine pigment, mating-system, biotic, abiotic and demographic processes

**Narbona, E., Wang, H., Ortiz, P. L., Arista, M., & Imbert, E. (2018).**
*Flower colour polymorphism in the Mediterranean Basin: occurrence, maintenance and implications for speciation.*
Plant Biology 20(Suppl 1): 8–20.
DOI: 10.1111/plb.12575.

### Supports

The review summarizes biochemical/genetic bases and multiple proposed maintenance mechanisms, including pollinators, non-pollinator selection, drift, gene flow, selfing and clonal reproduction. It also emphasizes geographic variation in morph composition.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use to support the Discussion boundary that the geometric axis does not identify a unique mechanism.

### Does not support

Its Mediterranean incidence estimates are not transferable to the 42,111-species frame and must not be used as global prevalence evidence.

## 4. White-versus-pigmented combinations have historical precedent, but that precedent is not a global mechanistic explanation

**Warren, J., & Mackenzie, S. (2001).**
*Why are all colour combinations not equally represented as flower-colour polymorphisms?*
New Phytologist 151: 237–241.
DOI: 10.1046/j.1469-8137.2001.00159.x.

### Supports

The paper reports that, in its British-flora survey, some anthocyanin-associated pink/purple/mauve versus white combinations were more common than some other colour combinations and discusses presence/absence of anthocyanin pigmentation as one possible biological context.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use only as a historical precedent that white/pigmented contrasts have long been noticed in flower-colour polymorphism.

The present third-cohort result is stronger in a different sense: it prospectively tests a fixed colour-space axis under a construction-preserving null.

### Does not support

Do not claim from this paper that:
- the present axis is caused by anthocyanin loss;
- white morphs universally arise by one-step mutations;
- the British flora pattern is global;
- the present q_white direction is a pigment assay.

## 5. Citizen-science photographs can support colour research, but image-derived colour requires explicit validation

**Laitly, A., Callaghan, C. T., Delhey, K., & Cornwell, W. K. (2021).**
*Is color data from citizen science photographs reliable for biodiversity research?*
Ecology and Evolution 11: 4071–4083.
DOI: 10.1002/ece3.7307.

### Supports

Citizen-science photographs can contain biologically useful visible-colour information, while uncontrolled photography adds variation and cannot recover spectral dimensions such as UV.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use to motivate validation of photo-derived colour rather than assuming that large image counts imply measurement validity.

### Does not support

Its sampling recommendations or validation performance must not be imported as validation of the current pipeline.

## 6. Geographical flower-colour mapping from iNaturalist photography has direct precedent

**Luong, Y., Gasca-Herrera, A., Misiewicz, T. M., & Carter, B. E. (2023).**
*A pipeline for the rapid collection of color data from photographs.*
Applications in Plant Sciences 11(5): e11546.
DOI: 10.1002/aps3.11546.

### Supports

The study used thousands of iNaturalist records for geographical flower-colour analysis and explicitly considered colour correction and tissue-selection challenges.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use as direct precedent for deriving floral colour measurements from community-science images and for separating measurement choices from ecological interpretation.

### Does not support

It does not validate the present automated ROI/palette system or the H2 axis.

## 7. High-throughput within-species floral colour extraction from iNaturalist images now has direct single-species precedent

**McKenzie, P. F., Church, S. H., & Hopkins, R. (2026).**
*High-Throughput iNaturalist Image Analysis Reveals Flower Color Divergence in Monarda fistulosa.*
The American Naturalist 208(1): 101–109.
DOI: 10.1086/739413.

### Supports

The published study reports high-throughput automated flower identification/colour extraction from more than 40,000 photographs and spatial colour divergence within a species.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use to position the current analysis as part of an emerging image-based floral phenotyping literature, while emphasizing that the current target is a multispecies species-level polymorphism phenotype and recurrent geometry.

### Does not support

A single-species pipeline does not validate the current multispecies measurement model.

## 8. iNaturalist observations have an observer process that must be treated as part of sampling

**Di Cecco, G. J., Barve, V., Belitz, M. W., Stucky, B. J., Guralnick, R. P., & Hurlbert, A. H. (2021).**
*Observing the Observers: How Participants Contribute Data to iNaturalist and Implications for Biodiversity Science.*
BioScience 71(11): 1179–1188.
DOI: 10.1093/biosci/biab093.

### Supports

Observer behaviour and specialization contribute structure to iNaturalist data.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use to motivate the H1 observer-disjoint validation design and the restricted population claim.

### Does not support

Observer splitting does not remove every platform, habitat, equipment or accessibility bias.

## 9. Validation splits must match the intended generalization target

**Roberts, D. R., Bahn, V., Ciuti, S., Boyce, M. S., Elith, J., Guillera-Arroita, G., Hauenstein, S., Lahoz-Monfort, J. J., Schröder, B., Thuiller, W., Warton, D. I., Wintle, B. A., Hartig, F., & Dormann, C. F. (2017).**
*Cross-validation strategies for data with temporal, spatial, hierarchical, or phylogenetic structure.*
Ecography 40: 913–929.
DOI: 10.1111/ecog.02881.

### Supports

Validation structure should be chosen to match the intended transfer claim; random splitting can overstate generalization when observations are structured.

### Allowed manuscript use

Use to distinguish:
- observer-disjoint validation;
- species-disjoint confirmation;
- independent-source replication.

### Does not support

It does not make the third cohort independent of iNaturalist-wide or shared-measurement-system error.

## 10. The minimum nonzero Monte Carlo p-value is a reporting resolution, not an exact exhaustive tail probability

**Phipson, B., & Smyth, G. K. (2010).**
*Permutation P-values should never be zero: calculating exact P-values when permutations are randomly drawn.*
Statistical Applications in Genetics and Molecular Biology 9: Article 39.
DOI: 10.2202/1544-6115.1585.

### Supports

With randomly drawn permutation/null replicates, plus-one Monte Carlo probabilities avoid zero p-values and have finite resolution.

### Allowed manuscript use

For the present 999-null H2 tests, p = 0.001 is the minimum attainable value under the frozen `(1 + #null >= observed)/1000` rule.

### Does not support

More null draws would change Monte Carlo resolution, not biological sample size or the exchangeability assumptions of the null.

## Manuscript positioning allowed by this audit

The Introduction may now make four externally supported background statements:

1. within-species trait variation can be an ecological object in its own right rather than only residual noise around species means;
2. flower-colour polymorphism is a long-standing evolutionary problem with multiple candidate maintaining processes;
3. image-derived floral colour from community-science photographs has direct empirical precedent;
4. community-science sampling and validation structure must be treated explicitly.

The Discussion may additionally state:

5. the present recurrent white/nonwhite geometry is compatible with a long history of white/pigmented flower-colour polymorphism observations, but it does not identify an anthocyanin mechanism;
6. the third cohort is a species-disjoint prospective confirmation within one source/opportunity universe, not an independent-source validation.

## 8. Post-audit mechanistic addendum — pigment accessibility and temperature

Date added: 2026-09-25 JST.

These sources were added after the original 2026-09-18 literature-positioning audit because the manuscript Discussion was revised to include a bounded mechanistic interpretation of the recurrent achromatic–chromatic axis and the post-confirmatory BIO5 result. They support interpretation only and do not alter any frozen empirical result.

### Wessinger & Rausher 2012 — multiple molecular routes and pleiotropic constraint

**Wessinger, C. A., & Rausher, M. D. (2012).**  
*Lessons from flower colour evolution on targets of selection.*  
*Journal of Experimental Botany*, 63(16), 5741–5749.  
DOI: 10.1093/jxb/ers267.

Permitted use:
- support the general statement that floral pigment loss can arise through multiple structural and regulatory routes;
- support the idea that pleiotropic consequences influence which molecular routes contribute to flower-colour evolution.

Not permitted:
- infer that the present white/nonwhite axis is caused by one anthocyanin mechanism;
- infer evolutionary transition direction from the present sign-invariant H2 statistic.

### Lacey 2026 — temperature dependence of floral colour

**Lacey, E. P. (2026).**  
*Temperature and the evolution of flower color: A review.*  
*American Journal of Botany*, 113(1), e70106.  
DOI: 10.1002/ajb2.70106.

Permitted use:
- support the general statement that floral anthocyanin and colour responses to temperature are widespread but context-dependent;
- motivate genotype, developmental-stage, light and other environmental modifiers.

Not permitted:
- convert the third-cohort BIO5 association into a universal temperature effect;
- treat literature plausibility as replication of the FCP environmental result.

### Narbona et al. 2026 — direct heat-linked white floral plasticity

**Narbona, E., Perfectti, F., González-Megías, A., Navarro, L., del Valle, J. C., Armas, C., & Gómez, J. M. (2026).**  
*Heat drastically alters floral color and pigment composition without affecting flower conspicuousness.*  
*American Journal of Botany*, 113(1), e70096.  
DOI: 10.1002/ajb2.70096.

Permitted use:
- provide a concrete within-species example in which warmer conditions are associated with a reversible lilac-to-white floral phenotype and altered anthocyanin/flavonoid composition;
- demonstrate mechanistic compatibility between thermal perturbation and an achromatic floral endpoint.

Not permitted:
- claim that *Moricandia* proves the cause of the global FCP H2 geometry;
- claim that all natural white-flower polymorphisms are thermally induced.

### Combined manuscript boundary

The manuscript may therefore state that the positive third-cohort BIO5 association is biologically compatible with temperature acting on floral pigment networks, while emphasizing that the fixed discovery/reserve transport test failed. The correct synthesis is **context-dependent thermal access to a recurrent pigment-network endpoint**, not a universal high-temperature whitening rule.

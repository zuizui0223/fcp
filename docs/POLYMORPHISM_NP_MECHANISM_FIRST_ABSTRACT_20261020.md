# Alternative New Phytologist manuscript framing — mechanism-first, evidence-bounded

**Provisional title:** Recurrent flower-colour axes and contrasting geographical and climatic organisation within plant species

**Status:** A revised narrative proposal based on completed data, not a replacement for the original frozen analysis; no direct population-genomic or experimental mechanism has yet been inferred from the global photographs.

## New Phytologist Summary (working, four paragraphs)

Intraspecific floral variation may reflect morphs coexisting within populations, differentiation among populations, or both. Yet global comparisons rarely distinguish the directions in phenotype space along which variants arise from the spatial and environmental processes that distribute them. We examined their relationship using flowering-plant photographs.

From 447,500 genuinely measured photographs of 4,475 species, 227,107 received classifiable four-state colour labels. A previously frozen, species-disjoint test confirmed a recurrent white–chromatic displacement direction (W = 0.517 versus a structured-null median of 0.457, P = 0.001).

Across 1,761 spatially eligible species, colour mismatch was lower within 50 km than across the sampled range (species-equal difference 0.0176; permutation P = 0.005). White–chromatic contrasts contributed 83.8% of this structure, but their proportionally normalized sorting was not stronger than that of other colours. In 1,574 species, warmer localities showed a small positive white-classification association (mean +0.0323 log odds °C⁻¹; genus-bootstrap 95% interval +0.0179 to +0.0473).

A recurring phenotype direction thus coexists with heterogeneous spatial organisation and a modest climatic association. Genetic population history, environmental selection and tissue-specific pigment regulation remain competing explanations rather than established global causes.

## What changes from the previous paper

**Original lead:** geographic colour clustering exists.

**New lead:** the same phenotype-space direction can arise and be geographically allocated without a universal colour-selecting environment. Distinguish (1) trait accessibility (the white–chromatic axis), (2) geography and ecological sorting (50-km depletion, IBD-like vs IBE-like, signed BIO5), and (3) mechanisms of local maintenance (C/S evidence and PAL/WAL tissue specificity).

This is **not** the claim that historical fragmentation was measured or proved dominant. Published case studies show that both genetic structure and environmental effects can matter and may govern distinct colour dimensions even within one species (e.g. Koski & Galloway 2020 on Campanula americana, DOI 10.3389/fpls.2020.00991). The manuscript must distinguish earlier prospectively frozen tests from post hoc five-cohort results and explicitly preserve the unconfirmed original BIO5 transport gate.

## Proposed Results order

1. **Worldwide repertoire of photographed within-species colour variation:** source sampling, colour-state composition and independently confirmed continuous white–chromatic direction. Do not misstate the 3,029 labelled multi-colour species as 3,029 genetically polymorphic populations.
2. **How colour variation is organized geographically:** within-50-km versus whole-range mismatch in five disjoint cohorts, 1,761 species, matched null and species distribution of effects.
3. **Geography vs present-day environment:** existing three-cohort phenotypic IBD-like and bounded BIO5 IBE-like turnover diagnostics, followed by the *distinct* positive average signed BIO5–white effect in 1,574 species. Temperatures are 1970–2000 normals at photo coordinates.
4. **The same white axis does not imply the same selective or molecular route:** 83.8% raw spatial component vs 79.6% baseline, normalized component non-difference. Clearly mark Silene F3h1/Myb1a, PAL/WAL frequency, Boechera drought/herbivory and Orychophragmus OvANS evidence as **external** mechanistic studies, not measured genotypes of the 447,500 photographs.
5. **Competing explanation tests and inferential ceiling:** history/connectivity versus environmental sorting versus time/biotic maintenance versus tissue-restricted pigment loss, with what has and has not been measured.

## Claims that remain hypotheses

- Geographic IBD-like correlation **cannot alone identify post-glacial range expansion, refugia or gene flow**.
- Positive white–BIO5 association **cannot alone establish local adaptation or a pigment savings benefit**.
- Species-disjoint continuous W alignment **does not identify a shared causal mutation, white-loss direction or artifact-free floral colour classification**.
- Literature-documented within-population C and among-population S **cannot currently be tested as an outcome by directly joining the strict 34-species literature table with the photographic 50-km results**: only five spatial matches, zero S-only.
- PAL / WAL pigment differences and Boechera fitness response illustrate why **different genetic/physiological routes** are plausible, not their worldwide causal frequency.

## Immediate expansion required for a mechanism claim

Link independent population-level evidence at a minimum in selected taxa: repeated wild-population colour frequencies, photometrically validated petal coloration, neutral genomic divergence, past range connectivity and contemporary environmental differences at the same sampled sites; then organ-specific anthocyanin chemistry and common-garden genotype × water/light treatments with fitness measurements. Test whether residual geographical sorting is explained by historical connectivity after controlling current environment and whether residual environmental sorting remains after matching ancestry and spatial distance.

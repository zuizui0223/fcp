# FCP: the white-versus-pigmented benefit–cost framework
## 2026-10-08 hypothesis ledger — source-backed biological framing, not a new outcome test

### Central evolutionary question

Why do visibly white and chromatic flower forms recur within species, and under what circumstances does each form persist across the geographic range?

The interesting generality is not "all coloured flowers are superior", "white is free" or "pigment synthesis is the universal limiting cost". Instead:

**A flower's pigmentation is potentially a trade-off between context-dependent reproductive / protective benefits and resource, antagonistic-interaction, mating-system and pleiotropic costs. Alternative colour states may be locally favoured in different environments, while the species as a whole retains both.**

The model is a *hypothesis* about phenotype payoffs, not a causal inference from the global photo atlas.

### Biological logic (distinguish local from range-wide maintenance)

For species i and true mating population j, write the *relative* coloured-versus-white phenotype payoff heuristically as:

    DeltaW(i,j) =
        benefit_pollination(i,j)
      + benefit_flower_and_vegetative_defence(i,j)
      + benefit_abiotic_stress(i,j)
      - cost_pigment_resources(i,j)
      - cost_colour_dependent_florivory(i,j)
      - cost_pleiotropy_or_mating(i,j).

This is a bookkeeping decomposition. Signs of the agent contributions are empirical and may reverse; one term may represent correlated pigmentation genotype rather than direct petal colour.

**If DeltaW has the same positive sign in all populations, the white morph is generally selected against. The mere simultaneous existence of a benefit and a cost does not produce stable polymorphism.** Species-wide persistence can instead arise when the *net sign differs among regions* with migration, when selective regimes change over years and genotype-dependent storage/seed banks matter, when morph success is frequency-dependent, or when mutant forms repeatedly originate. Stable genetic polymorphism *within* one population needs a balancing/segregation mechanism rather than mere exact cancellation at a constant environment, since exact neutrality need not yield stable equilibrium.

This is why geographically structured mostly homogeneous local photo neighbourhoods and occasionally mixed regions are conceptually informative, but can also arise from neutral drift/dispersal histories or photo classification artifacts.

### Literature anchors: directly measured trade-offs versus hypotheses

1. **Pigmentation/defence versus reproduction under resource context: _Boechera stricta_** — Vaidya et al. (2018), _New Phytologist_, DOI 10.1111/nph.14998, studied wild and common-garden purple vs white flowers. Purple morphs showed lower foliar damage and pigmentation could be induced by drought; white-flowered plants had a fecundity advantage in well-watered controls. This is direct support in ONE species for a countervailing ecological benefit, not universal carbon-synthesis cost.

2. **Organ-specific pleiotropy — _Silene littorea_** — Del Valle et al. (2019), _BMC Plant Biology_, DOI 10.1186/s12870-019-2082-6. Petal-only anthocyanin-loss plants reached 8–21% in two stable polymorphic populations; plants lacking anthocyanin throughout their photosynthetic tissues remained below 1%. HPLC discriminated flower-limited versus whole-plant pigment loss. This supports **organ-specific constraints on losing pigmentation**, NOT demonstrated metabolic savings of all photographed white flowers.

3. **Pollination benefits versus petal/leaf damage — _Anemone coronaria_** — Saabna et al. (2025), _Plant Biology_, DOI 10.1111/plb.13744. Red, purple and white morphs received different damage classes, but measured maternal fitness was not detectably different. Beetles can function as both pollinators and floral antagonists. The proposed service–damage balancing is plausible but no selection-by-pigment cost coefficient was directly identified.

4. **A distinct selective trade-off via mating: _Silene littorea_** — Discussion of Buide et al. 2021 in Saabna et al. (2025) reports pigmented pollination/herbivory advantages opposed by white-morph selfing reproductive success. This must be checked against the original independent paper before being used as a quantified FCP cross-species prior.

5. **Alternative to genetic balance: _Moricandia arvensis_** — Gómez et al. (2024), _Evolution Letters_, DOI 10.1093/evlett/qrae017, distinguishes maladaptive flower-colour plasticity from selection for summer flowering, which can maintain flower-colour change indirectly. Photo-derived coexistence therefore is not enough to prove maintenance of segregating colour alleles.

### What the current 1,499-species FCP photographic dataset actually identifies

- 149,900 source photographs cover 1,499 sampled species (500 + 500 + 499); 369/363/377 species clear the first eligibility gate for measured D.
- Four-category flower-colour photo variation is more locally homogeneous than same-species global photo composition, with 50-km mean within-species discordance depletion +0.020529/+0.018673/+0.014685, P=0.005 each.
- Under exact month-specific colour composition, 50-km local residual is +0.017455/+0.015287/+0.013324, P=0.005 each, and remains positive when observer IDs differ and when month×year is conditioned (PR #129, green Actions 37736541255).
- The new geographic atlas found no replicated, multiplicity-adjusted universal high-latitude / high-elevation increase in white-photo fraction or coarse colour diversity (PR #127, green Actions 37739944082). The regional atlas recovers robust mid/extratropical geographic structure where sampling is sufficient; tropical/polar domains are sparsely covered (PR #130, green Actions 37742381444).
- A separately prospective species-disjoint H2 white-versus-nonwhite photographic direction has W=0.517 versus structured-composition null median 0.457, P=0.001; this is a recurrent **measured phenotypic axis**, not a universal pigment genotype or adaptive mechanism.
- Under symmetric minimum five *per individual colour category* photo eligibility, white+one chromatic hue appears in 172/160/184 sampled species (total 516), while at least two distinct chromatic hues are observed in 55/51/60 (total 166); the categories overlap. These are **sample-construction- and measurement-dependent comparisons, NOT actual global prevalence or proof of a white-specific evolutionary maintenance advantage**.
- Crucially, after requiring BOTH white/nonwhite and nonwhite/nonwhite local-mixture opportunities in the same species, the paired 50-km white-specific versus other-hue relative-mixing contrast did **not** replicate as significant (exploratory P=0.5667/0.8123/0.2553). White-labelled photographs are also exposure-coupled (H2 clipping OR ~1.444). This blocks falsely treating raw 516-vs-166 photo counts as evidence for a global white morph fitness balance.
- Strict 10-km/14-day independent-observer photographic coappearance was found in 3 species among only 12 species with minimal same-year sampling opportunity (PR #128); neither genetic polymorphism frequency nor local long-term fitness balance is estimable.

### Three distinct tests of universality, in ascending causal strength

**Test A — phenotype-space accessibility (current cross-species photos partially support this):** after composition-aware nulls and independent image/organ review, is an achromatic–chromatic transition overrepresented relative to any single non-white-to-non-white axis? H2 answers a narrower conditional geometric variant. It does not prove cheaper mutations or smaller synthetic costs.

**Test B — geographic payoff mosaic (current cross-species photos support sorting, not the payoff):** do true within-species white-versus-chromatic transitions concentrate where the *relative* ecological advantages are predicted to change, rather than simply at the same absolute altitude/latitude globally? Needed: geography-matched environmental heterogeneity / pollinator or antagonist ecology, neutral demographic controls, independent photo-white validation and out-of-sample species transport. Prior same-species local W-C vs nonwhite-hue segregation data are inconclusive. Note that a whole-species 50-km clustering result cannot be assigned uniquely to white/pigment costs.

**Test C — actual local balance (not estimable from these images):** do pigmented and white forms cross over in germination/survival, viable seed fitness, selfing/inbreeding, pollination service, florivory and/or stress tolerance across environments and morph frequency? This requires replicated plants/populations, direct spectral/biochemical phenotype identity, known genotype/vegetative-pigment state, field reproductive output and neutral/phylogenetic controls. Studies can report the individual cost–benefit terms without assuming they all exist universally.

### Decision boundary for FCP

The current source-grounded large-scale inference is **a recurrent achromatic/chromatic photo-colour axis plus geographically distributed within-species visible colour variation in a taxonomically large, biased photo opportunity sample**. The benefit–cost equilibrium is a plausible, concrete *explanation to test*, not a discovered universal evolutionary rule.

Of the competing candidate mechanisms, the clearest biologically distinct hypothesis is **the low pleiotropic cost of regulating petal pigmentation independently of vegetative photoprotection/defence**. Unlike generic "pigment production costs ATP", it makes discriminating predictions about PAL versus WAL genotype/tissue-specific biochemistry, differential persistence, and field fitness. Source evidence currently comes mostly from _S. littorea_ and biased published tables (13 PAL / 13 WAL records), with only two overlaps of each biochemical class in an FCP image cohort — insufficient to promote global universality.

Do not rewrite frozen New Phytologist manuscript decisions or relabel FCP species as genetic populations. The legitimate contribution at this stage is identifying the benefit–cost **candidate axis** and defining what independent biological data would falsify it.

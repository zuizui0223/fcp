# What was measured? A cross-scale flower-colour measurement and inference contract

**FCP unified New Phytologist draft, 14 October 2026.** Source-preserving methods clarification; original photographs, classifier decisions, H1/H2 frozen endpoints and prospectively unexposed taxa are unchanged.

## 1. Research object and terminology

The primary empirical object is **photographic flower-colour variation**: observable colour states in community-science images and their variation **among named species, among photo locations within a species, and among repeated photographs of the same species**. This is not simply an atlas of average species colours. It also does **not** yet measure genetic flower-colour polymorphism sensu coexistence of discrete, heritable morphs among reproducing plants in one natural population.

Use the following terms consistently:

| Phrase | Actual observational unit and legitimate definition | Not established |
|---|---|---|
| Flower colour / flower hue | Visible-light camera image of a photographically detected/segmented floral region | Calibrated petal reflectance, UV nectar guides, pigment identity/concentration |
| Four-state photographed hue | Fixed white, yellow/orange, red/pink, blue/purple label on a classifiable flower photo | Genetic morph or an unbiased census of a species' modal phenotype |
| Continuous colour composition | Original nine-palette fractions of floral pixels after segmentation | Chemical concentration or independent human floral-organ annotation |
| Global photographic colour diversity | Different hue labels among source photos and nominal taxa | Global species-level *prevalence* of heritable morph polymorphism |
| Intraspecific sampled colour variation | Different photo hue labels or continuous colour measurements among photos assigned the same named source species | Verified independent genets, local coexisting breeding morphs, plasticity-versus-genetic causes |
| (D_i), species-wide hue diversity | (D_i=1-\sum_{k=1}^4 p_{ik}^2) calculated from classifiable high-depth photo proportions | Biologically unbiased population-morph diversity |
| Geographic photo-colour allocation | Pairwise colour concordance/discordance conditional on real photographed coordinates or original cells, with fixed-composition matched nulls | Local adaptation or breeding-population sympatry |
| Achromatic–chromatic direction (W) | Excess alignment of fitted within-species continuous two-mode colour-displacement vectors with a fixed white–nonwhite contrast under a construction-preserving null | White-pigment loss, the ancestral state, genetically homologous morphs, or environmental selection |
| Floral colour polymorphism (strict biological usage) | More than one heritable floral morph observed within natural reproductive populations, with individual/locale evidence | Cannot be counted from one image per species or broad cross-cell pairs |

**Recommended paper identity:** *a study of global-to-within-species photographic flower-colour variation and its spatial organization*. Flower colour is the **measured trait**, intraspecific variation is the **biological target**, and moisture-dependent pigment benefit–cost balance is a **testable ecological explanation**.

## 2. Specimen/photo selection and units

1. Source sampling drew iNaturalist Research Grade, species-rank photographs with flowering annotation and recorded photo/site/observer IDs, allowed Creative Commons licences, actual georeferenced locations, unobscured coordinates and original positional accuracy **≤5 km**. Discovery preceded flower-colour inspection. A fixed **18 × 9 equal-area grid** yielded **42,111 nominal taxa**; a metadata-only observer-capped scan identified **4,730** source taxa with historic opportunity for at least 100 retained photos. **This is a photographic opportunity census, not a probability sample of angiosperms**.
2. The original one-photo-per-taxon breadth programme classified **18,457/42,111** floral photo hues; **23,654** remained unknown. The separate taxon×geographic-cell photo programme had **85,337** records (39,075 classifiable). After deducting **26,905 overlapping photo IDs**, their union is **100,543 distinct image IDs**, of which **45,953** have classified four-hue outcomes. Counts of photo records, unique taxa and taxa×cell records must never be pooled.
3. The frozen worldwide cross-cell photo-pair frame preselected **13,416** different-observer, different-cell photos assigned to the same nominal taxon, before inspecting the paired colour outcome. Only **3,196** pairs have both hue labels classified; **797** are hue-discordant (24.94% conditionally). The missing 10,220 pairs make the broad-range frequency unidentified. These are geographic photographic differences, not observations of morph coexistence at one locality.
4. High-depth inference drew **500 discovery, 500 species-disjoint original validation and 499 fresh prospective taxa** from the same overarching metadata opportunity universe, using deterministic geographic-maximin observation allocation, maximum **two photos per observer per species**, exactly **100 raw photographs per measured species**, and source-ID exclusions. **1,499 species /149,900 photos** were measured. Only **1,109** met at least 40 four-hue classifiable-photo gates (369/363/377), **439** yielded primary eligible (W) displacement vectors across the cohorts (152/129/158), and only **158** belonged to the untouched pre-frozen *white-axis* prospective test. This is sampling *from one overarching metadata frame*, but the analytic response data/denominators are **not a nested narrowing of the climate-model sample**.

## 3. Physical and algorithmic flower-colour measurement

The biological measurement pipeline (source commit `9fae6ccdf684a46026f72ba12e98de2c5c54bf2a`) is **location blind** during the pixel pass. ROI-v4 locates a candidate flower region and EfficientSAM segments a flower mask; its visible image pixels yield a normalized **nine-coordinate palette** in fixed order:

`[white, yellow, orange, red, pink, magenta, purple, blue, bronze]`.

For descriptive ecological analysis, classifiable outcomes are collapsed to four fixed **photographic categories**: `white`, `yellow_orange`, `red_pink`, `blue_purple`. Outcomes failing flower ROI localization, segmentation, flower/foreground purity, visible-colour classification, or presenting a mixed/uncertain palette are **terminal nonclassifiable/missing outcomes**; ambiguous is not a fifth biological morph and no failure is imputed to white or to monomorphism. The original high-depth pipeline fixed these gates before biological outcome opening. The source macro study reuses the historically archived photo outputs; **some source image classes have measured nine-vector fractions despite ambiguous four-state classification** (PR #159) and must not be silently dropped or treated as true zeros.

This is a **digital camera visible-palette classification**, not a standardized-colour-card assay, calibrated spectrophotometry, UV-visible reflectance measurement or molecular anthocyanin/carotenoid assay. Illumination, organ detection and digital exposure matter. In the frozen high-depth third cohort, white versus digital near-clipping was associated (OR **1.444**, 95% CI **1.389–1.502**). The high-clipping exclusion kept numerical (W) support but missed its predeclared 90% retained-vector minimum (**142/158 = 89.9%**), leaving the formal direct-validity gate **INDETERMINATE**, not a clean biological-white PASS. No red/blue/purple photographic class is presumed an anthocyanin chemical measurement.

## 4. The defensible global → within-species bridge is measured on identical photos

The bridge is an **already completed source-only, retrospective test (PR #158)**, not a new reanalysis: compare an annual-rainfall white-photo gradient **among congeneric named species** to a contrast **within the same named species** *using exactly the same* **21,448 original photograph IDs, 6,143 species and 787 genera**. Both calculations use the **identical original classified four-state photo outcomes**, original source site BIO12 rain and site temperature/geography covariates; species photo responses are averaged/centred on the appropriate between/within level. The source does not share a single causal estimator across those levels. BIO12 is standardized by the original **736.416 mm photo-site source SD**.

| Same-photo comparison | Estimated change in original white-photo probability per source rainfall SD | Original matched genus bootstrap 95% interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| Among congeneric species | **+2.722 percentage points** | **+0.705 to +4.714** |
| Within named species across photographed sites | **−0.511 pp** | **−1.436 to +0.335** |
| Between minus within, paired difference | **+3.233 pp** | **+1.175 to +5.400** |

The original paired 1,999-genus-bootstrap one-sided p was **0.0005**, *exploratory and source-conditioned*. Within-species signed rainfall association does **not** receive affirmative support. This is the crucial interpretation: **a broad association between species' photographed floral colour and rainfall is not automatically a shared response among different flowers of the same species**. It does not reject lineage-dependent adaptation; it rules out declaring a single universally demonstrated photo-white/water mechanism from these observational scales. The headline 20,694-species +2.255 pp rainfall estimate is a **different regression/support and maxT family**, not numerically identical to the +2.722 same-photo bridge.

A second already performed check (PR #159) uses the **continuous white floral ROI-pixel fraction** on the same **14,667 images from 3,351 source species (489 genera)** and finds congeneric +2.229 pp/SD (95% +0.177 to +4.282), within-species −0.180 (95% −1.011 to +0.651), using **its own 717.253 mm photo-source rainfall SD**. That positive between-species trend **loses statistical support** under stricter ROI area/foreground-quality selection (+1.116, 95% −1.169 to +3.401), and is *not independent spectral validation*. The contrasting source Brier-score study (PR #156; identical 14,776 heldout photos) evaluates a different loss-based estimand and does not supply a significant scale contrast; keep in SI, do not conflate coefficients and predictive losses.

## 5. From photographic variation to its geometric and spatial organization

Once high-depth per-species photo fractions (p_{ik}) can be estimated reliably (≥40 classifiable photos), (D_i=1-\sum p_{ik}^2) reports **range-wide photo-colour diversity**, not coexistence in one local breeding population. Observer-disjoint 200 repeated photo splits supported its original H1 reliability, while same-system independent-photo transport among 136 species gave Spearman **0.968**.

For each H2 candidate species, valid nine-palette compositions were **normalized and Hellinger-transformed**; a frozen **deterministic unlabelled two-means** procedure obtained two fitted photo-colour modes. Require ≥0.10 (strict ≥0.20) coarse second state and continuous minor mode and a nonzero cluster-mean difference. Normalize this difference to a **sign-invariant direction (u_i)**; use the fixed white–equal-nonwhite contrast `q_white = normalize([1, -1/8, ..., -1/8])` and (W=\mathrm{mean}_i (u_i^\mathsf{T}q_{white})^2). A **999-world construction-preserving null** permutes nine-palette vectors across species *within each already-defined coarse colour* while holding every species×coarse-state photo count fixed, reclusters all worlds and recomputes W. Thus the null already retains the observed original coarse white versus nonwhite distinction; an observed excess tests **more than a simple classification artefact** under this specific null, though it cannot remove white-related photo exposure artefacts.

The 152 discovery +129 original-validation vectors only retrospectively localized the named white axis. The 158-vector separate high-depth third cohort tested the pre-frozen target **prospectively** (W 0.517245746 vs its own null median 0.457142815, p=0.001). **439** is the descriptive number of all already evaluable vectors; its weighted W ≈0.515557 versus weighted original separate null *means* ≈0.450362 is **NOT a pooled hypothesis test** and has no pooled p-value.

Geographic photo-colour structure is a separate level. Within each high-depth species, photo colour at nearby observed sites was compared to that species' overall photo-colour pair diversity, with **199 same-species count-preserving photo-location label nulls** (50 km primary; ≥30 local photo pairs). Source species equal-weight local depletion in three species-disjoint cohorts was **0.02053/0.01867/0.01468**, all source matched p=.005. The pattern survived removal of white photos, different-observer-only pairs and continuous nine-palette dissimilarity. Again this is not a census of individual genets.

## 6. What would make it genuinely *flower-colour polymorphism* biology?

Evidence of reproducibly different flower phenotypes **on independently verified flowering individuals** assigned to the **same natural breeding population**, preferably repeatedly observed in the same flowering season, is needed before declaring genuine local morph coexistence. Genetic inheritance, molecular pigment phenotype, temporal persistence and within-population allele frequencies require additional independent plant-level measurements. *Maintenance* by frequency-dependent or water-dependent selection additionally requires seed/pollen relative fitness under controlled environment and morph-frequency treatments, and proper genotype/family backgrounds.

**Main manuscript wording:** “global and within-species *photographic flower-colour variation*, including morph-like alternatives in repeated images”; use the strict term *flower-colour polymorphism* for literature precedent or verified natural-population examples, **not** the measured prevalence at the 42,111- or 1,499-species scale. Observed 46.95% white **among classifiable photos** has nonignorable missingness, species-conditioned regional evenness p=.214 and negative geographic compensation p=.997; these reject promoting an evolutionary 50:50 equilibrium from that descriptive ratio.

**Paper link in one sentence:** The world atlas shows which visible flower colours are documented and that different original photographs of the same named species sometimes differ; the same-photo bridge demonstrates that the global positive colour–moisture slope does **not** transport as a shared within-species slope; high-depth species-disjoint photographs establish a recurrent direction and spatial organization of within-species *photographic* variation; the ecology of pigment benefit/cost remains a **falsifiable mechanism hypothesis**, not an already confirmed cause.

# FCP spatial evolution evidence ledger — 2026-10-07

## Central biological question

What does range-wide intraspecific flower-colour variation mean biologically?

The key alternatives are:

1. colour variants coexist locally throughout a species' range;
2. species-wide diversity is generated mainly by geographic differentiation among relatively homogeneous localities;
3. geographic differentiation is mostly an isolation-by-distance/history pattern;
4. environmental differences add nonredundant sorting consistent with local adaptation or plasticity;
5. spatial floral divergence contributes to assortative mating and reproductive isolation.

The current FCP data can discriminate among the first four only to a bounded extent. They cannot measure fitness, gene flow or reproductive isolation.

## Literature prior

Population-level flower-colour work has long shown that FCP can be maintained by pollinators, abiotic selection, drift, gene flow, selfing and clonal reproduction. The Mediterranean synthesis by Narbona et al. (2018) emphasized that many polymorphic species consist mainly of monomorphic populations plus fewer polymorphic populations, producing geographic clines or mosaics.

More generally, plant balancing-selection theory distinguishes within-population maintenance by mechanisms such as negative frequency dependence from among-population adaptive polymorphism generated by spatially varying selection. Spatial environmental heterogeneity can maintain variation when local selection is sufficiently strong relative to migration, but reciprocal-transplant or equivalent fitness evidence is needed to distinguish adaptive differentiation from drift/history.

The IBD/IBE framework provides a useful diagnostic distinction: geographic separation can structure divergence through dispersal/history, while environmental differences can add structure beyond distance. Here this logic is applied to phenotype distance and must be described as **phenotypic IBE-like sorting**, not genetic isolation by environment.

## Evidence status

### E1 — species-wide FCP is geographically distributed rather than uniformly locally mixed

**Status: SUPPORTED, strongest new ecological result.**

At the preregistered post hoc primary 50-km radius, local pairwise four-state diversity was lower than expected from each species' fixed range-wide composition in all three species-disjoint cohorts:

- discovery: n=166, species-wide pair diversity 0.22139, local 0.20087, depletion **0.02053**, matched p=**0.005**;
- validation: n=181, species-wide 0.22866, local 0.20999, depletion **0.01867**, p=**0.005**;
- third: n=204, species-wide 0.24820, local 0.23352, depletion **0.01468**, p=**0.005**.

The same local-depletion direction is supported at 25, 100 and 250 km in all three cohorts.

Composition-preserving vertex nulls fix coordinates and every species' exact colour-state counts. Therefore the result is not a consequence of high-D species merely having more possible colour differences.

**Allowed claim:**

> Species-wide flower-colour variation is geographically partitioned: nearby conspecific observations contain less flower-colour diversity than expected from the species' overall colour composition.

### E2 — the distributed-polymorphism result survives direct measurement/artifact challenges

**Status: SUPPORTED.**

At 50 km the local-depletion result remains positive in all three cohorts when:

1. all same-observer photo pairs are excluded;
2. all white observations are removed and only yellow/orange, red/pink and blue/purple remain;
3. coarse states are abandoned and continuous nine-colour Jensen–Shannon dissimilarity is used.

Different-observer depletion:
- discovery 0.01998, p=.005;
- validation 0.01690, p=.005;
- third 0.01457, p=.005.

Nonwhite-only depletion:
- discovery 0.01947, p=.005;
- validation 0.00729, p=.025;
- third 0.00891, p=.040.

Continuous nine-colour local depletion:
- discovery 0.01518, p=.005;
- validation 0.01366, p=.005;
- third 0.01301, p=.005.

Seasonal and cross-year falsification tests are recorded separately when complete.

**Interpretation:** geographic partitioning is not restricted to the exposure-coupled white classifier, not restricted to four coarse categories, and is not generated simply by comparing photos from the same observer.

### E3 — species with higher D are more strongly locally partitioned

**Status: MIXED / SCALE-DEPENDENT; do not make a primary claim.**

At the primary 50-km scale:
- discovery rho=0.0390, p=.135;
- validation rho=0.1171, p=.025;
- third rho=0.1118, p=.005.

The association is supported in all three cohorts at 25 and 250 km, but not cleanly at 50 or 100 km under the discovery–validation replication rule.

**Conclusion:** retain the older D–spatial association as evidence that diversity and spatial organization covary, but do not claim a scale-invariant law that high-D species allocate a larger fraction of diversity among localities.

### E4 — broad environmental heterogeneity explains between-species D

**Status: NOT SUPPORTED as a general explanation.**

The primary multivariate climate-heterogeneity predictor did not replicate discovery → validation.

A solar-radiation heterogeneity signal survived the 500+500 screen but failed fixed transport to the 377-species third cohort:
- third partial rho=0.0171, p=.382.

**Rejected claim:** species-wide flower-colour diversity is generally generated by greater climatic heterogeneity.

### E5 — BIO5-associated phenotypic IBE-like sorting

**Status: REPLICATED IN RAW CONTINUOUS FLOWER COLOUR, BUT TECHNICALLY BOUNDED.**

Using continuous nine-colour flower dissimilarity:

- discovery: rho(colour, BIO5 | geography)=**0.00925**, p=.005;
- validation: **0.00826**, p=.010–.015 depending on deterministic null seed implementation;
- third: **0.01019**, p=.005.

Thus BIO5 difference adds a small nonredundant association with flower-colour turnover after geographic distance is removed.

However:
- validation flower-minus-background differential is not supported: mean rho=-0.00328, p=.82;
- strict same-observer-pair sensitivities are unsupported in all three cohorts.

**Allowed claim:**

> A small BIO5-associated colour-turnover component is reproducible in the raw continuous flower measurements after geography is controlled.

**Claim ceiling:**

> Current controls do not establish that this component is flower-specific, observer-independent, causal or adaptive.

### E6 — IBD-like versus IBE-like structure

**Status: BOTH SUPPORTED; IBD-LIKE COMPONENT IS LARGER.**

Continuous nine-colour results:

Discovery:
- IBE-like mean partial rho=0.00925, p=.005;
- IBD-like mean partial rho=0.02760, p=.005.

Validation:
- IBE-like 0.00826, p=.010;
- IBD-like 0.02414, p=.005.

Third:
- IBE-like 0.01019, p=.005;
- IBD-like 0.02225, p=.005.

In every cohort, mean IBE-IBD is negative. Descriptive commonality partitions likewise give larger unique geographic than unique BIO5 components.

**Allowed synthesis:**

> Flower-colour turnover contains a dominant geographic-distance component plus a smaller BIO5-associated component that is not reducible to distance alone.

Do not say that environment is stronger than geography.

### E7 — one universal directional temperature response

**Status: NOT SUPPORTED.**

The earlier signed prediction that white states occupy warmer BIO5 environments passed in the third cohort but failed the frozen discovery/validation transport rule and weakened under observer controls.

Combined with E5, the current pattern is compatible with **directionally heterogeneous environmental responses**: temperature-associated colour turnover can recur without every species changing toward the same colour.

Because E5 fails important technical sensitivities, this remains a hypothesis rather than a main causal conclusion.

### E8 — local adaptation

**Status: UNRESOLVED.**

The current FCP data contain no direct fitness measurements, reciprocal transplants, common gardens or population-genomic evidence.

The strongest observational precursor is:
- repeated geographic partitioning of FCP;
- dominant IBD-like structure;
- a smaller technically bounded IBE-like component.

Local adaptation would require showing that local colour phenotypes have higher local fitness, or that genotype–environment associations exceed neutral population structure.

### E9 — reproductive isolation / speciation

**Status: NOT TESTABLE WITH CURRENT DATA.**

Prior flower-colour systems show that colour divergence can alter pollinator behaviour and assortative mating, and in Mimulus/Phlox can contribute to reproductive isolation. FCP can therefore be a precursor or component of diversification.

The present photo dataset does not measure pollinator-mediated mating, hybridisation or gene flow. The correct use is as a forward hypothesis:

> geographically partitioned floral ITV creates the spatial phenotype structure on which divergent pollinator or abiotic selection could act, but reproductive isolation is not demonstrated.

## Recommended manuscript hierarchy

### Main ecological result

**Species-wide flower-colour ITV is geographically distributed.**

This should replace the generic statement “higher D is more spatially organized” as the clearest biological result. It directly states where ITV resides.

### Supporting spatial-process result

Both IBD-like and IBE-like components are detected, with IBD stronger. Because the BIO5 component fails background/observer robustness, place its strongest interpretation in Discussion/SI rather than use it as proof of adaptation.

### Complementary phenotype-space result

The prospectively confirmed achromatic–chromatic H2 geometry remains valuable as a separate answer to **which direction variation occupies**, but should not carry the entire ecological interpretation.

### Main unresolved evolutionary question

Does the geographic partitioning represent:
- divergent local adaptation;
- phenotypic plasticity;
- migration–selection balance;
- drift/colonisation history;
- or combinations of these?

That question is now sharply defined by the comparative pattern and can be tested with field fitness/genomic/common-garden data.

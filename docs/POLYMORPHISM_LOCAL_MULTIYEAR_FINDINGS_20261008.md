### Key observed evidence

The already species-disjoint, classifiable photographed flora has 547 species containing >=5 visually white records and >=5 nonwhite records (184/167/196). Their local, multi-observer, multi-year recurrence is NOT globally estimable from these 547 because the images were selected geographically widely.

| Maximum photograph pairwise diameter | Species with necessary >=6 local photo and two-year/two-observer opportunity among 547 | Species with <=diameter white+nonwhite, >=3 each, each documented in >=2 years and by >=2 observers |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10 km | 45 | 15 |
| 25 km | 100 | 40 |
| 50 km | 170 | 87 |

At 50 km, opportunity fraction is 170/547, so the 460 species without strict recurrence cannot be called true local-monomorphism cases. Of the 90 species with enough both-colour near-photos at 50km, 87 also clear the multi-year/multi-observer gate. This shows that the principal bottleneck is local photo density and local colour opportunity, not necessarily failure of time/observer recurrence.

The 50-km two-nonwhite-hue strict photo recurrence count was 8/4/9 species in discovery/validation/third; these totals use their *own* species eligibility denominators. In paired within-species eligibility (both white+nonwhite and two nonwhite hues possible), the 50-km exact discordance p-values are 0.6875/0.125/0.03125, respectively. The third result is neither a replicated excess across cohorts nor multiplicity-corrected and MUST NOT be promoted to biological white-morph preference.

A complementary observer-independent, distance-matched local white/nonwhite discordance contrast (different-year minus same-year) was +0.0043 / +0.0114 / -0.0002 in the three cohorts. All species-bootstrap intervals include zero. No consistent seasonal or temporal morph replacement is detected; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence of fluctuating fitness.

### Mechanistic counterexample already present in FCP candidates

The validated candidate ledger includes Moricandia arvensis (taxon 165527). Literature:
- Gómez et al. 2020 Nature Communications DOI 10.1038/s41467-020-17875-1: one individual changes from lilac spring to white summer flowers, tracking seasonal environment, and pollinator guild changes.
- Gómez et al. 2024 Evolution Letters DOI 10.1093/evlett/qrae017: this floral polyphenism is not itself locally adaptive even if plants bearing both floral forms produce more seeds; selection for summer flowering indirectly maintains it.
- 2025 AJB paper PMID 40926385: white summer petals can accumulate high UV-absorbing phenolics rather than merely lacking all pigment.

The pre-defined exploratory photograph audit of all 53 classifiable northern-hemisphere validation photos found spring 20 white / 10 nonwhite, summer 3 white / 0 nonwhite, 20 other-month photos. A prespecified >=5 photos each in spring/summer required for a one-sided season contrast FAILED, and there was no near-site two-season photo comparison. The positive-control photographic test is therefore NOT ESTIMABLE. Do not interpret the apparent fraction of white photos in spring as evidence against the field experiments: garden status, observer selection, white-exposure bias, geography and sampling are unverified.

### Ecological take-home and next necessary distinction

The observational result establishes **a set of geographically proximate, repeatedly photographed white+nonwhite states**, not genetically segregating morphs, fixed population coexistence or selection.

The biologically meaningful causal possibilities must be kept separate:
1. genetic variation in petal-specific pigmentation, potentially retained by balancing or heterogeneous selection;
2. nonadaptive floral plasticity as a correlated by-product of selected growth/phenology traits;
3. light, camera or colour-classifier error; 
4. geographically separated populations within the coarse photo diameter.

Without independent biochemical/genomic/morph identity evidence and fitness, the three-cohort photo patterns cannot decide among these pathways. Avoid turning a descriptive map into proof of pigment adaptation or selection for white petals. The next strongest ecological evidence is not another unbounded environmental scan but a source-backed independent organism/morph/phenology criterion, tested on truly local sites with repeated individual or genetic identity.

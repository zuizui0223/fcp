### Question 1 — What hypotheses or questions does this work address?

When a species is variable in flower colour across its range, does that variation coexist locally or is it partitioned among geographic localities? Does geographic turnover reflect distance/history alone or also environmental differences, and are there recurrent directions of within-species phenotype-space displacement?

### Question 2 — How does this work advance our current understanding of plant science?

Across three species-disjoint high-depth cohorts, nearby conspecific observations contain less flower-colour diversity than expected from each species' fixed range-wide composition. This distributed-polymorphism pattern survives exclusion of same-observer pairs, removal of white records and continuous nine-colour reanalysis. It generalizes, across hundreds of plant species, the clinal and mosaic population structure previously documented in individual flower-colour systems.

### Question 3 — Why is this work important and timely?

ITV is increasingly treated as a species property, but a species-wide diversity value does not reveal where variation resides. Our results show that flower-colour ITV is spatially allocated: a species can be diverse across its range while local neighbourhoods are comparatively homogeneous. This creates the population structure on which spatially varying selection, gene flow, drift or plasticity can act, while avoiding the stronger claim that local adaptation has already been demonstrated.

## Key result and evidential chronology

The study begins from an outcome-blind **42,111-species iNaturalist opportunity frame**. A high-depth resource contains **500 discovery + 500 species-disjoint validation species and 100,000 photographs**; a later cohort adds **499 newly sampled species and 49,900 photographs** after the achromatic–chromatic target was frozen.

The central ecological result is a post-outcome analysis that was transported across all three species sets. At the fixed 50-km scale, mean species-wide minus local four-state pair diversity was **0.02053** in discovery, **0.01867** in validation and **0.01468** in the third cohort; a composition-preserving matched null gave **p = 0.005 in all three**. The effect persisted when same-observer photo pairs were excluded, when every white observation was removed, and when continuous nine-colour dissimilarity replaced the coarse states. The same local depletion was also detected at 25, 100 and 250 km.

Continuous colour turnover contained both geographic and environmental structure. Geographic distance remained associated with colour after BIO5 difference was removed (mean partial rho = **0.02760**, **0.02414**, **0.02225** across the three cohorts), while BIO5 difference retained a smaller association after geography was removed (**0.00925**, **0.00826**, **0.01019**). We describe the latter only as a phenotypic IBE-like candidate because matched-background and strict same-observer controls do not confirm it as a flower-specific, observer-independent environmental response.

A complementary prospectively protected result concerns phenotype space. The original 1,000 species localized a recurrent achromatic–chromatic axis; after freezing that target, **158** species in the new cohort yielded W = **0.517** versus a coarse-state-preserving structured-null median of **0.457** (p = **0.001**). This remains evidence for recurrent displacement geometry, not for one universal adaptive cause.

## Scope boundary

The prospective cohort is species-disjoint but was drawn from the **same iNaturalist opportunity universe** and processed with the same measurement system; it is **not an independent-source replication**. The fresh-image D transport check is likewise same-source/same-system and is not presented as external validation. The direct highlight control shows that the measured coarse white state is exposure-coupled rather than artifact-cleared. High-clip exclusion retained H2 support, but retained 142/158 primary vectors (89.9%), one vector below the prespecified 90% validity threshold, so the frozen executable control remained formally **INDETERMINATE**. The study therefore does not interpret the white state as free of image-formation effects and does not identify pigment chemistry, evolutionary direction, pollinator selection or climatic adaptation. Fresh validation tests showed no detectable broad tree-wide phylogenetic conservation of D, while the discovery association with sampled photographic span collapsed essentially to zero.

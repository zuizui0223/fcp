We submit the Full Paper, **“Within-species flower-colour variation shows recurrent achromatic–chromatic geometry across plant species,”** for consideration in *New Phytologist*.

### Question 1 — What hypotheses or questions does this work address?

Can flower-colour polymorphism be measured reproducibly as a species phenotype, does it repeatedly align with a common colour-space direction, and do more polymorphic species organize colour variation more strongly across geography rather than merely reflecting sampling extent or broad ancestry?

### Question 2 — How does this work advance our current understanding of plant science?

We extend distributional approaches to intraspecific trait variation by separating three properties of the same within-species phenotype distribution—amount, direction and geographic organization—and asking whether they carry distinct cross-species information. Across 149,900 photographs, displacement repeatedly aligns along an achromatic–chromatic axis, while more diverse species show stronger spatial organization.

### Question 3 — Why is this work important and timely?

Flower-colour polymorphism is usually studied within populations, while broad comparative analyses often compress species to scalar summaries. Our results show that retaining the distribution is not enough unless its properties are distinguished: species can share the **direction** of variation, and variation **amount** can covary with spatial **organization**, even when the environmental coefficients and geographic maps organizing that variation are context dependent.

## Key result and evidential chronology

The study begins from an outcome-blind **42,111-species iNaturalist opportunity frame**. A high-depth discovery–validation resource contains **1,000 species and 100,000 photographs**, and an untouched prospective confirmation cohort adds **499 species and 49,900 newly sampled photographs** after the target axis was frozen. A later fresh-image execution also strengthened the H1 measurement claim: across **136** overlapping species, earlier and fresh D estimates showed Spearman rho = **0.968** and Lin CCC = **0.972**. This is same-system transport, not independent-source replication.

The prospective cohort completed **49,900** terminal measurements from 499 species, with **377** species passing the predeclared measurement-support gate. At the primary tier, **158** species yielded W = **0.517** versus a coarse-state-preserving structured-null median of **0.457** (p = 0.001); the strict tier gave W = **0.533** (p = 0.001). Thus the prospective result confirms an increment above the construction-controlled baseline rather than the entire white-axis signal relative to isotropy. No species were replaced and no axis, threshold or null was refitted after biological opening. A subsequent one-shot direct highlight control reacquired all 49,900 frozen image rows without source drift. Near-clipping was positively coupled to white classification within species (OR = **1.444**, 95% CI **1.389–1.502**), while exclusion of the response-blind high-clip set retained H2 support (W = **0.503**, p = 0.001).

The chronology is central to the manuscript. The original discovery and validation cohorts localized the recurrent construction-controlled geometry to a fixed white-versus-nonwhite axis only after the broad geometry had been opened. We therefore treated those analyses as target discovery/localization, froze the axis and inferential machinery, and tested the target prospectively in a new species-disjoint cohort.

A complementary frozen analysis provides the main ecological clue: greater species-level D is associated with stronger within-species geographic colour organization in both original cohorts. In validation, the relationship persists after sampled-span and clear technical-failure adjustment (partial rho = 0.0993, p = 0.025), remains positive for a matched flower-minus-background response (rho = 0.1162, p = 0.010), and survives uniform ambiguity-endpoint stress tests. Taken together with H2, this yields the central biological contrast of the paper: **what varies is partly recurrent across species, whereas whether where that variation is sorted is shared or species-specific remains unresolved**. We interpret this as a structural ecological pattern and an open spatial question, not as evidence for one universal causal mechanism.

## Scope boundary

The prospective cohort is species-disjoint but was drawn from the **same iNaturalist opportunity universe** and processed with the same measurement system; it is **not an independent-source replication**. The fresh-image D transport check is likewise same-source/same-system and is not presented as external validation. The direct highlight control shows that the measured coarse white state is exposure-coupled rather than artifact-cleared. High-clip exclusion retained H2 support, but retained 142/158 primary vectors (89.9%), one vector below the prespecified 90% validity threshold, so the frozen executable control remained formally **INDETERMINATE**. The study therefore does not interpret the white state as free of image-formation effects and does not identify pigment chemistry, evolutionary direction, pollinator selection or climatic adaptation. Fresh validation tests showed no detectable broad tree-wide phylogenetic conservation of D, while the discovery association with sampled photographic span collapsed essentially to zero. Secondary evidence provides bounded mechanistic clues rather than a universal cause. A structured extraction of published *Silene littorea* RNA-seq/HPLC results localizes one high-frequency PAL system near F3h1/Myb1a regulation; our frequency-table reanalysis then shows substantially higher reported natural frequencies for flower-restricted than whole-plant anthocyanin loss. By contrast, a prospective-cohort BIO5–white association weakens under observer controls and fails fixed species-disjoint transport, so temperature remains an observer-sensitive contextual clue.

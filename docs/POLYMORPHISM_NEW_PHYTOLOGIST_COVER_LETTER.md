Dear Editors of *New Phytologist*,

We submit the Full Paper, **“A recurrent achromatic–chromatic axis structures within-species flower-colour polymorphism across plant species,”** for consideration in *New Phytologist*.

The manuscript addresses a simple but unresolved question: can within-species flower-colour polymorphism be measured reproducibly as a species-level phenotype across many plant species, and, if so, is that variation geometrically arbitrary or repeatedly aligned with a common direction in colour space?

The study separates measurement validation, target discovery and prospective confirmation. First, a continuous four-state polymorphism score was reproducible across 200 observer-disjoint reserve partitions, with a median split Spearman correlation of 0.789 and a fifth percentile of 0.765. Second, analyses of the original discovery and reserve cohorts localized the recurrent construction-controlled geometry to a fixed white-versus-nonwhite axis rather than to a general shared hue direction. Because that named axis was identified after the original broad geometry had been opened, we did not treat those analyses as untouched confirmation. Instead, we froze the axis, statistic, admissibility thresholds, support gate and structured null before opening a new species-disjoint cohort.

The prospective cohort completed **49,900** terminal measurements from 499 species, with **377** species passing the predeclared measurement-support gate. The already frozen white-versus-nonwhite direction was supported at both the primary tier (**158** species, W = **0.51725**, structured-null p = 0.001) and the prespecified stricter tier (**86** species, W = **0.53293**, p = 0.001). No species were replaced and the axis, statistic, thresholds and null construction were not refitted after biological opening. The terminal verdict was `H2_PROSPECTIVE_WHITE_AXIS_CONFIRMED`.

We think the work is well suited to *New Phytologist* because it is plant-wide, question-driven and focused on a general property of intraspecific phenotypic variation rather than on a single taxon or locality. Community-science image collections increasingly make it possible to study trait distributions rather than only species means, but scale alone does not establish measurement validity. Our design explicitly separates observer-disjoint validation from target discovery and then from prospective species-disjoint confirmation, providing both a biological result and a transferable framework for macro-scale intraspecific trait analysis.

We also preserve the limits of that inference. The prospective cohort is species-disjoint but was drawn from the **same iNaturalist opportunity universe** and processed with the same measurement system; it is **not an independent-source replication**. The study does not estimate global polymorphism prevalence and does not identify pigment chemistry, evolutionary direction, pollinator selection or climatic adaptation. Broad tree-wide phylogenetic signal was not supported in the reserve cohort, and a discovery association with sampled photographic span did not replicate; these negative results are retained as scope boundaries rather than replaced by additional predictor searches.

The manuscript is accompanied by five main figures, one cohort-role table, a frozen claim ledger, machine-readable prospective results and Supporting Information that records the full chain of custody from pre-opening selection through the terminal prospective test.

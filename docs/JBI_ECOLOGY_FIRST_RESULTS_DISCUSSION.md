# JBI ecology-first Results and Discussion text

Status: manuscript-ready ecological rewrite, 2026-09-03.  
This text does not replace frozen numerical outputs. It reorganizes their interpretation around competing ecological hypotheses.

## Results — competing ecological expectations

### Geographic differentiation occurred toward narrower occupied climatic niches

The five frozen climatic summaries showed a common direction. For a one-standard-deviation increase in each metric, the odds ratio for geographically structured rather than within-population flower-colour variation was below one for temperature breadth (OR = 0.817), moisture breadth (OR = 0.412), climatic heterogeneity (OR = 0.681), PCA dispersion (OR = 0.712) and PCA hull area (OR = 0.577). Thus, the observed direction was opposite to the simple broad-gradient environmental-sorting expectation, under which geographically differentiated cases should occupy climatic niches at least as broad as locally polymorphic cases, and instead matched the broad-niche coexistence expectation that locally coexisting variation is associated with broader species-level ecological occupancy.

Moisture breadth showed the largest individual contrast (OR = 0.412, 95% CI = 0.180–0.947; family-clustered Wald `p = 0.0368`; permutation `p = 0.0423`). This single-axis threshold evidence weakened after multiplicity correction (Holm-adjusted Wald `p = 0.184`; permutation `p = 0.212`) and under finite-cluster and phylogenetic treatments. The repeated direction, rather than moisture alone, was more stable: every leave-one-family-out estimate for all five metrics remained below one, and both Open Tree and time-scaled phylogenetic analyses retained negative point estimates across all five metrics, although their confidence intervals included one. For moisture breadth, CR2/Satterthwaite inference gave OR = 0.407 and `p = 0.063`.

These results therefore identify a comparative ecological pattern rather than a uniquely resolved climatic axis. In the present documented sample, geographic flower-colour differentiation was associated with narrower sampled climatic occupancy, but the data do not establish that moisture or climatic breadth itself causes the spatial organization of colour variation.

## Results — ecological mechanism discrimination

### Large-scale spatial climatic partitioning did not explain geographic differentiation

A later display-core-v6 screen tested whether the comparative pattern could be reduced to simple large-scale spatial environmental structure. Among 32 C/S-informative species, the three spatial-climate diagnostics were all unresolved. Pairwise environmental turnover gave S versus C OR = 1.37 (family-bootstrap 95% CI = 0.49–7.51), normalized climate-centroid separation between major geographic sectors gave OR = 1.62 (0.56–3.48), and regional Gaussian climate overlap gave OR = 1.00 (0.48–3.36). Total climatic hypervolume also failed to produce a clean ordered distinction (S versus C OR = 2.08, 0.46–6.79; C+S versus C OR = 0.47, 0.26–1.16).

These results do not support a simple model in which geographically differentiated flower-colour systems are characterized by stronger separation among broad macroclimatic regions. The primary 34-species breadth result therefore cannot be reinterpreted as a trivial consequence of S species occupying more strongly partitioned large-scale climates.

### Static climate seasonality provided one fragile clue

Mean occupied temperature seasonality (BIO4) was the only v1 mechanism screen with an S-versus-C family-bootstrap interval excluding one (OR = 0.349, 95% CI = 0.085–0.909), consistent with greater climatological seasonality in locally coexisting systems. However, the interval included one after adding mean temperature, absolute latitude or both as controls (ORs = 0.412–0.458). Precipitation seasonality did not show the predicted direction (S versus C OR = 1.50, 0.41–5.15).

We therefore treat temperature seasonality as a candidate clue rather than as evidence for a temperature-seasonality mechanism. Climatological BIO4 also cannot distinguish repeated seasonal cycles from actual year-to-year environmental turnover, which is the temporal process of direct ecological interest.

### Fragmentation remained plausible but unresolved

Multiscale geographic fragmentation was directionally associated with S and C+S relative to C (S versus C OR = 1.80; C+S versus C OR = 1.75), but both family-bootstrap intervals were broad and included one. Geographic fragmentation therefore remains a plausible connectivity modifier but is not supported as an explanation by the present range proxy. In particular, a range-fragmentation metric is not a measurement of pollen or seed gene flow.

## Discussion — ecological interpretation

### Broad climatic gradients are not the simplest explanation for geographic colour differentiation

The main ecological result is a directional contrast between two competing expectations. If broad macroclimatic gradients generally create opportunities for spatially varying selection and replacement of colour variants, geographically differentiated species should occupy climatic niches at least as broad as locally polymorphic species. Instead, all five frozen estimates pointed in the opposite direction: geographically structured cases occurred toward the narrower end of sampled species-level climatic occupancy. That direction persisted when individual plant families were removed and under two phylogenetic treatments, even though uncertainty increased enough that individual intervals often included one.

This pattern is more compatible with a broad-niche coexistence expectation than with simple broad-gradient sorting. One interpretation is that species occupying broader ecological conditions can maintain multiple phenotypes locally through frequency dependence, multiple selective agents or fine-scale heterogeneity, whereas geographic differentiation may arise within relatively narrow macroclimatic settings through local environmental differences, restricted connectivity, demographic history or drift. The data do not distinguish among those mechanisms, but they narrow the class of explanation: geographic colour structure does not appear to require a species to span an unusually broad macroclimatic niche.

### Spatial arrangement of environmental variation matters more than niche volume alone

The later mechanism screen sharpens this interpretation. Geographically differentiated cases did not show stronger large-scale climatic turnover, greater separation between major geographic sectors or lower regional climatic overlap. Total climate-space volume was also not a clean discriminator. Together, these results suggest that the ecological problem is not adequately summarized by the amount of climate space occupied by a species.

The more relevant distinction may be **how environmental heterogeneity is allocated through space and time**. Persistent spatially localized selection could generate geographic differentiation even within a relatively narrow species-level climate envelope, while temporally varying conditions could favour continued local coexistence if different colour variants gain relative advantages at different times. Biotic turnover and population history provide parallel routes to the same spatial outcomes. This shifts the next mechanistic question from `how broad is the niche?` to `where and when do selective environments differ?`.

### Temporal environmental heterogeneity is the strongest unresolved climatic hypothesis

The static BIO4 screen makes temporal heterogeneity a concrete candidate, but it is not itself a temporal test. A prospective TerraClimate analysis was therefore frozen before dynamic outputs were inspected, using the same 1,320 fixed locations through 1958–2025 and five direct metrics of seasonal or interannual environmental movement and variance. Its first exact execution did not reach modelling because remote extraction was cancelled before completion. Under the predeclared decision rule, that run is not evaluable rather than unsupported.

Accordingly, the present paper can state that temporal heterogeneity is the highest-priority unresolved climatic mechanism, not that it has already been demonstrated. A completed exact dynamic test would discriminate this hypothesis without changing the current primary result: the 34-species comparison establishes the direction of the ecological pattern, while the dynamic analysis asks whether temporal environmental variation can account for that pattern.

### Independent image evidence supports spatial organization but not a universal geography

A separate six-species held-out image analysis provides independent evidence that continuous flower colour can be geographically organized within species. Neighbouring observations were more similar than expected under species-conditioned random labelling (`p = 0.0113`, with the same direction at two predeclared graph sensitivities), whereas the stronger hypothesis that independent species concentrate their strongest transitions in the same global regions was not confirmed by the frozen primary test (`p = 0.0906`).

This result is conceptually consistent with the comparative finding: flower-colour variation can be spatially structured without requiring one universal global boundary or one broad macroclimatic sorting mechanism. Because the six-species image analysis and 34-species literature comparison use different samples, phenotypes and null models, they should be presented as convergent evidence streams rather than pooled statistically.

## Ecology-first conclusion paragraph

The current evidence points to a specific ecological asymmetry. Flower-colour variation is spatially organized within species, but geographically differentiated cases do not occupy broader species-level climatic niches and do not show stronger large-scale climatic partitioning in the available comparative data. Instead, geographic differentiation occurs toward narrower sampled climatic occupancy, while total niche size and coarse spatial environmental structure provide little discrimination. This pattern argues against a simple universal model of broad climatic gradients sorting colour variants and shifts attention toward finer-scale spatial selection, temporal environmental heterogeneity, biotic turnover and population history. Those mechanisms remain alternatives to be distinguished, not causal conclusions already established.
